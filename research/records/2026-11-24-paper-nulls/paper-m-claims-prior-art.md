## Claim 1 — Credential inflation is a positional mechanism: the credential–occupation gap should predict fertility better than attainment, especially in saturated countries

| closest prior work | DOI | status | new vs replicated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spence (1973), *Job market signaling*, QJE 87(3) | 10.2307/1882010 | V | Replicated (theory): signaling is the canonical positional account; our test is an empirical falsification of one operationalisation, not a new theory. |
| Arrow (1973), *Higher education as a filter*, J Public Economics 2(3) | 10.1016/0047-2727(73)90013-3 | V | Replicated (theory): screening/filter account. |
| Collins (1979), *The Credential Society* (2019 Columbia reissue) | 10.7312/coll19234 | V | Replicated (theory): credential inflation as social closure; used to motivate the gap measure. |
| Bills (2003), *Credentials, signals, and screens*, Review of Educational Research 73(4) | 10.3102/00346543073004441 | V | Replicated: the review already distinguishes credential, signal and screen; our result is consistent with its warning that these are hard to separate empirically. |
| Hungerford & Solon (1987), *Sheepskin effects in the returns to education*, REStat 69(1) | 10.2307/1937919 | V | Replicated (method family): diploma effects exist; not a fertility test. |
| Jaeger & Page (1996), *Degrees matter: new evidence on sheepskin effects*, REStat 78(3) | 10.2307/2109960 | V | Replicated (method family). |
| Weiss (1995), *Human capital vs. signalling explanations of wages*, JEP 9(4) | 10.1257/jep.9.4.133 | V | Replicated: the human-capital/signaling distinction is standard; our horse race is its fertility analogue. |
| Chevalier et al. (2004), *Does education raise productivity, or just reflect it?*, Economic Journal 114(499) | 10.1111/j.1468-0297.2004.00256.x | V | Replicated: the productivity-versus-signal test is established; our "gap is collinear with attainment" result is a measurement critique in the same spirit. |
| Leuven & Oosterbeek (2011), *Overeducation and mismatch in the labor market*, Handbook of the Economics of Education 4 | 10.2139/ssrn.1771245 (SSRN version) | S | **Closest to new:** the overeducation literature already flags that credential and job measures are hard to separate; our pre-registered finding that the gap is a noisy residual (r = 0.807 with attainment) is a direct measurement replication in a new outcome (fertility). |
| Bol & van de Werfhorst (2013), *Educational systems and the trade-off between labor market allocation and equality of educational opportunity*, Comparative Education Review 57(2) | 10.1086/669122 | V | Context: institutional sorting; not a competing test. |

**Novelty statement.** The theory and the wage-side evidence are replicated; the new element is a
pre-registered fertility-side test of the gap measure with explicit sign and saturation
falsifiers. The result is a measurement critique, not a refutation of signaling: attainment and the
credentialed-jobs share correlate +0.81 (r = 0.807, n = 46, re-verified from the committed panel),
so the gap cannot play the independent role the framework assigns it.

## Claim 2 — The massification drivers (electricity, paid employment, the schooling bundle) are general processes

| closest prior work | DOI | status | new vs replicated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gauthier (2007), *The impact of family policies on fertility in industrialized countries*, PRPR 26(3) | 10.1007/s11113-007-9033-x | V | Replicated context: policy/development effects are small and bounded; consistent with our null on paid employment and electricity once trends are allowed. |
| Thévenon (2011), *Family policies in OECD countries: a comparative analysis*, PDR 37(1) | 10.1111/j.1728-4457.2011.00390.x | V | Replicated context. |
| Luci-Greulich & Thévenon (2013), *The impact of family policies on fertility trends in developed countries*, Eur J Population 29(4) | 10.1007/s10680-013-9295-4 | V | Replicated context: small effects; our group-specific results are compatible with effect heterogeneity. |
| Frejka & Sobotka (2008), *Fertility in Europe: diverse, delayed and below replacement*, Demographic Research 19 | 10.4054/demres.2008.19.3 | V | Replicated: heterogeneity of fertility regimes; motivates the grouping test. |
| England, Budig & Folbre (2002), *Wages of virtue*, Social Problems 49(4) | 10.1525/sp.2002.49.4.455 | V | Replicated (care context): care employment is real and gendered; our null is on its independent fertility effect. |
| Kleven, Landais & Søgaard (2019), *Children and gender inequality: evidence from Denmark*, AEJ: Applied 11(4) | 10.1257/app.20180010 | V | Replicated (rival channel). |

**Novelty statement.** The discipline result — that education–income "laws" are between-country
regularities that weaken inside countries — is itself a replication of the levels-versus-changes
caution in the comparative literature; the new element is the 400-random-grouping yardstick with
empirical p-values and a pre-registered null that no driver is robust everywhere. The paper should
report this as a replication with a stronger null, not as a discovery.
