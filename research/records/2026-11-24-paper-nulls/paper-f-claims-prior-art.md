*For the consolidated Fertility paper section "The fertility mechanism under test". Each claim carries
3–8 closest works. DOI status: **V** = DOI resolved directly at Crossref; **S** = Crossref

## Claim 1 — The care share of female employment rises with the credential pipeline, and amplifies the schooling–fertility link

| closest prior work | DOI | status | new vs replicated |
|---|---|---|---|
| England, Budig & Folbre (2002), *Wages of virtue: the relative pay of care work*, Social Problems 49(4) | 10.1525/sp.2002.49.4.455 | V | **Replicated (partly):** the care penalty and the growth of care employment are established; our cross-country care-share gradient (8.1% → 28.4% across attainment terciles, n = 148) is a descriptive replication, not a new finding. |
| Budig & England (2001), *The wage penalty for motherhood*, ASR 66(2) | 10.2307/2657415 | V | Replicated: the care/motherhood penalty is the closest mechanism literature; our null on the *fertility* amplification is new in this cross-country form. |
| Kleven, Landais & Søgaard (2019), *Children and gender inequality: evidence from Denmark*, AEJ: Applied 11(4) | 10.1257/app.20180010 | V | Replicated: child penalties in earnings are well established; the care loop adds a fertility-side test the child-penalty literature does not. |
| Kleven et al. (2019), *Child penalties across countries*, AEA P&P 109 | 10.1257/pandp.20191078 | V | Replicated: cross-country child penalties; we use it as a rival channel, not a new result. |
| Del Boca (2002), *The effect of child care and part time opportunities on participation and fertility decisions in Italy*, J Pop Econ 15(2) | 10.1007/s001480100089 | S | Replicated: childcare availability–fertility link; our null says the cross-country care *sector share* is not an independent amplifier once income and urbanisation are in — a scope correction. |
| Thévenon (2011), *Family policies in OECD countries: a comparative analysis*, PDR 37(1) | 10.1111/j.1728-4457.2011.00390.x | V | Context: policy typology; used to interpret care-sector measures. |
| Gauthier (2007), *The impact of family policies on fertility in industrialized countries*, PRPR 26(3) | 10.1007/s11113-007-9033-x | V | Context: small policy effects; our care-loop null is consistent with the "policy inside the pipeline" reading. |
| Luci-Greulich & Thévenon (2013), *The impact of family policies on fertility trends in developed countries*, Eur J Population 29(4) | 10.1007/s10680-013-9295-4 | V | Context: policy effect sizes; not a competing test of the care loop. |

**Novelty statement.** The *descriptive* care-share gradient is replicated; the *causal* claim that
the care share amplifies the schooling–fertility link is not supported, and the placebo test (male
care and manufacturing) is, to our knowledge, not a test this literature has run in this form. The
new contribution is the placebo-based falsification, not the care gradient.

## Claim 2 — The strong no-off-switch claim: pipeline-compatible policy cannot restore sustained fertility above ~1.6

| closest prior work | DOI | status | new vs replicated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gauthier (2007), PRPR 26(3) | 10.1007/s11113-007-9033-x | V | Replicated: the review literature already reports small, positive, bounded policy effects; our bound test turns that into an explicit falsifiable bound. |
| Thévenon (2011), PDR 37(1) | 10.1111/j.1728-4457.2011.00390.x | V | Replicated: policy typology; used to define "pipeline-compatible" instruments. |
| Thévenon & Gauthier (2011), *Family policies in developed countries: a "fertility-booster" with side-effects*, Community, Work & Family 14(2) | 10.1080/13668803.2011.571400 | V | Replicated: bounded positive effects; our event study's +0.035 (SE 0.025) is within the range this literature reports. |
| Luci-Greulich & Thévenon (2013), Eur J Population 29(4) | 10.1007/s10680-013-9295-4 | V | Replicated: positive but small effects; consistent with our weak form, not the strong form. |
| Frejka & Sobotka (2008), *Fertility in Europe: diverse, delayed and below replacement*, Demographic Research 19 | 10.4054/demres.2008.19.3 | V | Replicated: the post-socialist fertility reversal and recovery patterns are documented; our long-run crossings (Bulgaria, Romania, Czechia) replicate them and they are what falsifies the strong claim. |
| Zakharov (2008), *Russian Federation: from the first to second demographic transition*, Demographic Research 19 | 10.4054/demres.2008.19.24 | V | Replicated: Russia's 2007 maternity capital and later rise are documented; our Russia event (6 years >1.6, max 1.78) replicates the pattern. |
| Koytcheva & Philippov (2008), *Bulgaria: ethnic differentials in rapidly declining fertility*, Demographic Research 19 | 10.4054/demres.2008.19.13 | V | **New finding in this form:** Bulgaria's 10 years above 1.6 after its 2005 package is the clearest counterexample to the strong bound; the descriptive series is documented, the pre-registered persistence test is new. |
| Tsuya (2015), *Below-replacement fertility in Japan*, in Low and Lower Fertility (Springer) | 10.1007/978-3-319-21482-5_5 | V | Replicated: Japan's policy record and continued decline; our Japan events (none above 1.6) replicate that. |
| Lowest-Low Fertility and Policy Responses in South Korea (2015), Springer chapter | 10.1007/978-3-319-21482-5_6 | S | Replicated: Korean policy expansion and continued decline; used as a case, not a new test. |
| OECD Family Database (family benefits, leave, childcare) | no DOI (database) | N | Context: policy intensity source for the wider project; this section's event dates are hand-coded, not OECD-verified. |

**Novelty statement.** The *weak* form is replicated (policy effects are small and bounded). The
*strong* form is **newly falsified**: the pre-registered persistence test shows 9 of 38
introductions followed by runs of 3–10 years above 1.6, which the existing evaluation literature
did not frame as a bound test. The contribution is the explicit bound and its falsification, not a
new claim that policies raise fertility.

## Claim 3 — Terminal enclosure behaves as a critical transition with early-warning signals

| closest prior work | DOI | status | new vs replicated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scheffer et al. (2009), *Early-warning signals for critical transitions*, Nature 461 | 10.1038/nature08227 | V | Replicated (method): the EWS toolkit is standard; we apply it to a demographic transition, which is the new substrate. |
| Scheffer et al. (2012), *Anticipating critical transitions*, Science 338 | 10.1126/science.1225244 | V | Replicated (method). |
| Scheffer et al. (2015), *Generic indicators of ecological resilience*, Annu Rev Ecol Evol Syst 46 | 10.1146/annurev-ecolsys-112414-054242 | V | Replicated (method). |
| Dakos et al. (2008), *Slowing down as an early warning signal*, PNAS 105 | 10.1073/pnas.0802430105 | V | Replicated (method). |
| Dakos et al. (2012), *Methods for detecting early warnings of critical transitions*, PLoS ONE 7(7) | 10.1371/journal.pone.0041010 | V | Replicated (method: trend + surrogate testing, which we implement). |
| Hastings & Wysham (2010), *Regime shifts in ecological systems can occur with no warning*, Ecology Letters 13 | 10.1111/j.1461-0248.2010.01439.x | V | **Replicated (skeptical side):** the contestation is established; our demographic null joins it. |
| Ditlevsen & Johnsen (2010), *Tipping points: early warning and wishful thinking*, GRL 37 | 10.1029/2010gl044486 | V | Replicated (skeptical side): distinguishes "early warning" from wishful inference; our wrong-sign autocorrelation is consistent. |
| Kéfi et al. (2013), *Early warning signals also precede non-catastrophic transitions*, Oikos 122 | 10.1111/j.1600-0706.2012.20838.x | V | Replicated (specificity critique); relevant because we cannot build a no-transition placebo (only 1 country). |
| Boettiger, Ross & Hastings (2013), *Early warning signals: the charted and uncharted territories*, Theoretical Ecology 6 | 10.1007/s12080-013-0192-6 | V | Replicated (limits); used to interpret the null and the MDE. |
| Dakos et al. (2015), *Resilience indicators: prospects and limitations*, Phil Trans R Soc B 370 | 10.1098/rstb.2013.0263 | V | Replicated (limits). |

**Novelty statement.** The method and the skeptical literature are replicated; the application to
national fertility transitions with a paired pseudo-event null and a 16,000-surrogate test is the new
element. The result is consistent with the skeptical side of the EWS literature, so it should be
reported as a replication in a new substrate, not a discovery about critical transitions in general.
