=== C1b: is the demand-first result an artefact of data availability? ===
median first year with any supply measure: 1975 (education spending/pupil-teacher/enrolment series mostly start around then)
schooling take-offs at the 1965 window boundary: 61 of 108
fair-comparison sample (schooling after 1965 and supply data present before the take-off): 33 countries
  supply before schooling: 76%; demand before schooling: 79%; supply before demand: 39%
  median gaps: supply-schooling -5 yrs; demand-schooling -10 yrs
demand measured only by wage-premium and paid-employment (no income/urbanisation): precedes schooling in 7% of 58 countries
  Reading: the broad development series (income, urbanisation) are the earliest-moving demand proxies;
  when only labour-market demand is used, demand-first weakens. Both sides' onset dates are also limited
  by their data start years, and the 1965 boundary censors early schooling dates, so the timing test
  is biased against supply. That is why the horse race below matters more than the raw ordering.

=== reading ===
raw timing: supply precedes schooling in 28% of countries, demand in 73%.
fair sample: supply 76%, demand 79% (both sides often turn together).
labour-market-only demand: precedes schooling in 7%. The raw demand-first ordering is
carried by broad development indicators (income, urbanisation), not by labour-market demand.
Horse race: supply block dominant (composite +0.456, SE 0.174); demand block zero (+0.006, SE 0.203).
=> the SUPPLY-LED / institutional (formatting) reading survives; the labour-market human-capital
reading is not supported by the timing of wage premia and paid jobs. The broad-development demand
proxy also leads, so this is not a clean separation: it is timing plus conditional association,
not causal identification (policy and income are jointly determined; no instrument here).
