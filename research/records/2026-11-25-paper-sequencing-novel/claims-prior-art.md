# Claim-by-claim prior art: the sequencing fact

Scope: the single claim of Paper N1 — that in late-industrialising countries the schooling take-off precedes
the release of agricultural labour, systematically and by decades, and that the supply side of massification
(public provision, compulsory schooling, state capacity) dominates labour-market demand.

## The novelty core, answered explicitly
**No paper was found that codes schooling take-off against agricultural/farm-exit event ordering on a common
country panel and reports the lead distribution (31 forward and 3 reverse among 34 countries with an identified order, 30 undetermined; both-exact median lead 18 years).** The
direction of the historical relation is well known and has been argued since the 1980s; the systematic
event-ordering compilation appears to be a new assembly. The paper says exactly that and never says "first".

## Why the paper differs from the closest competitor
Porzio, Rossi and Santangelo (2021) is the paper a referee will name first, and it is genuinely closer than the
enrolment compilations: it takes the schooling-structural-change relation seriously and gives it a model. What
it does not do — and what this paper does — is put both event dates on the same country panel and report the
distribution of leads: 31 of 34 identified countries forward (30 undetermined), median 18 years where both dates are exact, with the reverse in 3. That is an
assembly-and-measurement increment, not a new direction, and the paper's framing follows the auditor's line: the
paper systematises a known historical pattern; it does not discover the direction.

## What was ruled out, so it is not re-run
World-polity/institutionalism (works 2-7): the direction has been argued since 1985, but none of those works
measures event ordering. Economic-history sequencing case studies (Sandberg, Mitch, Goldin & Katz, Schleunes,
Flora, Diebolt): they establish the historical cases, which is what the historical section uses them for.
Human-capital demand theory (Schultz, Becker, Mincer, Goldin & Katz, Acemoglu & Autor): background, no
event-ordering measurement.

## The two vulnerabilities a referee will push on
1. **The date-by-date source table.** Every event date in the panel is traceable to the records named in the
claims-to-source map, but a published paper needs a country-by-country source table with the series and
derivation for each date, and this draft names the records rather than reproducing the table in the text.
2. **Alternative-threshold recoding.** The take-off rules are thresholds (the fastest sustained ten-year fall,
the minimum fall sizes, the common 1965-2010 window). A referee will ask what happens under other thresholds,
and the honest answer is that the forward share is reported under the pre-registered rules only; a threshold
sensitivity sweep is future work and is listed as such rather than asserted to be robust.
