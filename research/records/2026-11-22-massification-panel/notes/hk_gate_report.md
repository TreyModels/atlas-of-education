- The top tier is stable at about 1.4% for 5* or better and about 0.1-0.2% for 5** x5.

**4. Grading.**
- HKEAA's SRR booklet (`https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/doclibrary/media/leaflets/hkdse_srr_a4_booklet_jun2011.pdf`, P) says levels are reported against "explicit and fixed standards". Standards were set in 2012 and carried forward.
- Each year, cut scores are suggested by a latent-trait model that maps the prior year's cuts onto the new exam (core subjects) or by a Group Ability Index (electives). They are then settled by expert grading meetings and the Public Examinations Board.
- There are **no fixed percentages for levels 1-5**.
- Within level 5, **5\*\* is roughly the top 10% of level-5 candidates and 5\* the next roughly 30%**. So the top split is relative by construction.
- Consequence: broad tutoring could in principle lift the share reaching level 3 or 2. It cannot lift the 5**/5* share inside level 5. The 3322 share rose from 38.9% (2013) to about 42% (2016) and has been flat since, so there is no sign of score inflation.

**8. Tutoring surveys.** These are not comparable across years.
- Lee 1996 phone survey: 34% of middle-secondary and 41% of upper-secondary students (cited in Bray and Kobakhidze 2015; S).
- 2004/05 household expenditure survey, n=6,100 households: Grades 10-11 33.6%, Grades 12-13 48.1% (cited in Wang and Bray 2016; S).
- Caritas 2010: 81.9% middle and 85.5% upper secondary (cited in Bray and Kobakhidze 2015; S).
- Bray et al. 2011/12: Grade 9 **53.8%**, Grade 12 **71.8%**. This is a previous-12-months measure, 16 schools, n=1,646, with Band One schools at 74.2% (`https://ejournal.um.edu.my/index.php/JICE/article/download/2555/771/7734`, P).
- HKFYG 2023: 342 students, a convenience sample with no year stated on the page, 72.2% "still attending" (`https://cps.hkfyg.org.hk/2023/06/27/a-snapshot-of-private-tutoring-in-hong-kong/`, P).
- I found **no representative post-2015 time series**, and no revenue or centre counts. The nearest figure is 6,754 non-formal-curriculum institutions registered in Dec 2013, falling to about 1,200 after consolidation (S, via Wang and Bray 2016).

**9. Academic claims.**
- Wang and Bray 2016 argue that exam-based sorting sustains tutoring. They do not mention the HKDSE or SRR.
- I found **no peer-reviewed paper** showing the HKDSE got harder or that tutoring inflated scores. I also found none showing the opposite.
- HKEAA's secretary general said in 2017 (S, SCMP) that the qualifying rate was still above 2012-15.
- The explanation for the 2024 offer-rate drop (SCMP, Ng) is that more students cleared the core benchmarks, not that the exam got harder.

## Verdict

1. "Positional floor rising" is **not supported over 2013-2023**. DS candidates fell about 40%, the 3322 share was flat at 42%, and 3322-eligible candidates per funded place fell from 1.8 to 1.2 (computed).
2. Some tightening is visible from 2025, but not in the formal floor. 3322/332A-eligible candidates per place kept falling (1.23 in 2024, 1.09 in 2025, 1.06 in 2026), because the eligible share dropped. Day-school sitters per place rose from 2.71 (2024) to 2.89 (2026, computed), JUPAS applicants rose 9% (2025) and 5% (2026), and the offer rate fell from about 40% (2022) to 34% (2026). That is mostly demographic rebound and fewer students going overseas (S: 6.5% of F6 leavers in 2025, against about 6,000 in 2020). At the same time DSE share of intake fell from 80% to 74% (S) as non-local admissions grew.
3. "Concentrated in the top tier" is **weakly supported at most**. Admitted-student scores for MBBS and the LLB are flat, and one business programme rose. Top-scorer counts rose (16 to 24) while the 5** x5 count was flat at about 50, and SRR fixes the 5**/5* split relative to level 5.
4. Confounds: demographic decline, the CSD change (a laxer fourth core subject), SRR standard-setting that decouples reported grades from tutoring, self-financed and subsidy-scheme places (undocumented), non-local and non-DSE entrants, outbound study, and provisional 2025-26 figures.
5. Tutoring prevalence data are too sparse and non-comparable to test an arms race, so the claim remains untested rather than refuted.
