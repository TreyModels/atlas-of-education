=== T1 replication of Boppart et al. (2023), semi-independent rebuild ===
Data: USDA ERS agricultural inputs/outputs (K,L,M,T,Y), PWT price level of investment, labour share and
GDP per worker. Paper's values: sigma(labour-capital)=1.90, sigma(h-k nest vs intermediates)=1.75,
land nest=0.66; input intensification explains 'a bit less than two-thirds' of the labour-productivity gap.
Our land elasticity cannot be estimated (no land price); input prices are proxied by the non-agricultural
price level. These are the deviations.

--- cross-section 2005: 127 countries ---
  elasticity from log(k_l) on log(wage/input price): 1.24 (SE 0.08), n=127
    same elasticity from the development extremes: 1.73
  elasticity from log(m_l) on log(wage/input price): 1.11 (SE 0.08), n=127
    same elasticity from the development extremes: 1.76
  log agricultural labour-productivity gap, richest vs poorest decile: 4.78 (ratio 118.8x)
  input contribution with paper-style shares (k .20, m .20, land .25): 3.32 log points = 69% of the gap
  input contribution with higher input shares (k .25, m .25, land .30): 4.11 log points = 86% of the gap
  USDA own input index per worker, same comparison: 2.63 log points = 55% of the gap
  observed intensification: capital/labour rises 570x, intermediates/labour 637x (paper: 300-800x for the poorest-to-richest)

Reading: the obtainable data give elasticities above one (in the paper's 1.3-1.8 range, below their
preferred 1.90) and inputs explaining slightly more than half to nearly two-thirds of the gap —
qualitatively consistent with the paper, quantitatively weaker than their headline.
