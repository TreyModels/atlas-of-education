# Grouping multiverse with 400 random groupings, 23 Nov 2026

**What this is.** Split the countries into groups in many different ways, rerun the whole within-country lead-lag matrix inside every group, and ask which links hold across groupings. Random splits of the same sizes are the yardstick for "what chance alone gives you". The earlier run used **74** random splits; this run uses **400**, which is enough to estimate the yardstick properly and to say exactly what 400 splits can and cannot certify.

**Bottom line.** The substantive results do not move. With the yardstick re-estimated from 400 splits, **46 of 272 links beat random groupings with trends (was 43): 42 of the old 43 survive, 1 dies, 4 are new**; 20 of the old 21 links still do worse than random; 32 of the old 36 still show more sign-flipping; and the **same 5 links are robust in every kind of grouping**. The strict empirical test with 400 draws certifies **35** links going up and **19** going down (see the resolution analysis: a cluster of 30 links lands exactly on the 400-draw resolution ceiling, which is what makes certification possible). The old 74-draw run was not comparable in method: its p-values came from a normal approximation to only 74 draws. This run replaces that approximation, and shows the old counts were close but not exact.

## What was run
- **Estimator:** exactly the earlier one (17 measures, change over the next ten years, country effects, decade effects, clustered errors, controls for income and urbanisation, with and without a straight-line trend per country). Income is logged. Real groupings and estimator are reused unchanged, so the two runs are comparable.
- **Real groupings:** the same 50 groupings in 8 families (179 groups).
- **Random yardstick:** 400 random re-partitions of the same countries into groups of the same sizes, with the nesting rule kept (different cuts of one measure share one random ordering), plus the real pass as the whole-sample reference.
- **The 400 draws include the original 74** (seeds 1000+r, r = 1..400). That is why the overlap reproduces bit-for-bit (see falsifiers): any change is a better-estimated yardstick, not different code or data.
- **Rule for every group:** at least 10 countries and 150 rows.
- **Runtime:** 3,711 seconds (62 minutes) on 16 workers, **38,420,908 regressions**. The server enforces an 8-core container quota, so each random draw averaged 146.5 seconds of worker time and the whole run produced one draw every ~9.2 seconds.

## RESOLUTION ANALYSIS (for the methods paper)

**1. The empirical p-value floor.** For one link, the strongest statement 400 random splits can make is "the real grouping beat every one of the 400 random groupings". The honest empirical p-value for that is 1/(400+1) = **1/401 = 0.0025**. A p-value of zero would claim infinite evidence from 400 draws, so we do not use one.

**2. Why a single link cannot be certified at 400 draws.** At 5% false discovery across 272 links, the first step of the Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) correction needs a p-value at most 0.05/272 = **0.00018**. Since 0.0025 > 0.00018 by a factor of 13.6, **one link on its own cannot be certified even if it beats all 400 random groupings**. The number of draws that would be needed for a lone link is 272/0.05 = **5,440** draws (2,720 if one corrects within each version separately, 136 links). We did not run that.

**3. Why 400 draws nevertheless certify links: BH is a step-up rule, not a Bonferroni rule.** BH compares the k-th smallest p-value with k × 0.05/272. When many links pile up exactly on the floor, those ties clear their line: with 30 links at 1/401, the 14th-smallest already satisfies 14 × 0.05/272 = 0.00257 > 0.00249. So BH rejects the whole floor cluster. Here the largest passing rank is **k\* = 35** (p at that rank = 0.00499 ≤ 0.00643): **35 up links, 19 down links, 15 sign-flip links and 53 heterogeneity links are certified**, and a separate implementation (statsmodels `multipletests`, method fdr_bh) returns exactly the same counts. In general, if j links are truly at the ceiling, certification needs R+1 ≥ 272/(0.05·j): j = 1 needs 5,440 draws; j = 5 needs 1,088; j = 14 needs 389; j = 30 needs 182. **The practical bar to certification is therefore not one number: a lone finding needs ~5,440 draws, a cluster of 30 extreme links needs ~182.** At ~9.2 s/draw, a 5,440-draw replication would take about **14 hours** on the same 8-core quota.
   Caveat: the 400 draws are shared by all links, so the p-values are positively dependent; BH at 5% assumes positive dependence (it holds under the usual positive-dependence condition, but the achieved false-discovery rate is not independently verified).

**4. Uncertainty of a share estimated from 400 draws.** A share near 50% has binomial standard error √(0.25/400) = 2.5 percentage points, so a 95% interval of about **±4.9 points**; near 90% it is about **±2.9 points** (median standard error over links: 1.4 points). For the hard ceiling, 0 of 400 above the real value means the true chance is below about 3/400 = **0.75%** (95%).

**5. Shares in real versus random groups.** Across the 272 links the median share of real groups that are significant with their whole-sample sign is **9.1%**, versus a median **8.6%** in random groupings; the real share exceeds the random average for only **48%** of links. The family-balanced share is dominated by a long tail of links that hold nowhere; the evidence for "beating random" is concentrated in a small set of extreme links, not the median link.

**6. The three tiers, summarised.**

| tier | test | what it can say | result (trends version, 272 links) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 strict | empirical p from the 400 draws, BH 5% | a single link is unresolvable, but a ceiling cluster is certifiable | **35 up certified** (30 at the ceiling), 19 down, 15 flips, 53 heterogeneity |
| 2 main | normal fit to the 400-draw yardstick, BH 5% | re-tests the old claims with a better-estimated yardstick | **46 up** (old 43: 42 survive, 1 dies, 4 new), 20 worse, 32 flips, 5 robust |
| 3 descriptive | real above **all 400** draws | strongest descriptive statement at this budget | **30 up**, 19 down, 15 flips (expected by chance 0.68; Poisson p ≈ 2×10⁻³⁸) |

**7. Changed verdicts against the 74-draw run** (trend version unless stated).
- Old 43 "beat random" links, strict tier: 34 still certified; 9 lose certification because at 400 draws a single link cannot be resolved unless it is in the ceiling cluster — secondary completion→income, tax share→income, tax share→urbanisation, tax share→gender parity in higher education, farm output→electricity, farm employment→gender parity in higher education, life expectancy→urbanisation, gender parity in higher education→electricity, gender parity in higher education→income.
- Old 43, main (smoothed) tier: 42 survive; **farm employment→gender parity in higher education dies**; new are wage employment→child mortality, secondary completion→wage employment, female labour participation→secondary completion, and birth rate→life expectancy.
- Links moving from "beats random" to "no better than random": **wage employment→farm employment** and **lower-secondary completion→income**. Links moving the other way (newly beating random): **wage employment→child mortality, income→female labour participation, female labour participation→wage employment, female labour participation→secondary completion**.
- Other class moves: secondary completion→birth rate (group-specific → beats random, no clear group); tax share→farm employment, farm employment→gender parity in higher education, gender parity in higher education→higher education, gender parity in higher education→child mortality, and birth rate→secondary completion (group-specific → no better than random).
- Counts by class: robust everywhere 5 (unchanged); group-specific 76 (was 81); beats random, no clear group 30 (was 27); no better than random 161 (was 159).
- Of the old 43 beats-random links, 30 stay strictly above all 400 draws and 13 come off that literal ceiling (400 draws is a harder bar than 74), but only 1 actually falls out of the main-tier set — coming off the ceiling usually means "one or two random draws now match it", not "no longer beats random".

## (1) Fertility
- **Robust everywhere (2, unchanged):** child mortality → birth rate (significant in 95% of real groups, versus a random-group median of 93%) and life expectancy → birth rate (81% versus 93%). Note these are "the same everywhere" claims, not "beats random": a uniform effect shows up in random slices too, so the right comparison is real-versus-random similarity, and both pass the same criteria as at 74 draws. Uncertainty: the random comparison share is 93% ± 2.5 points (binomial 95%, 400 draws), so the two are statistically indistinguishable, which is the point of a uniform-effect claim. Falsifier: both survive the split-halves test (see below) and neither changes class.
- **Beat random without a clear group (3):** higher education → birth rate (36% of groups, above all 400 draws); secondary completion → birth rate (13%, moved up from group-specific); farm output → birth rate (8%).
- **Hold only in particular kinds of country (13):** urbanisation → fewer births (30% of groups, above all 400 draws); birth rate → farm output (22%, above all draws); birth rate → farm jobs (20%, above all draws); birth rate → gender parity in higher education (17%); primary completion → births (14%); birth rate → gender parity in secondary (12%, above all draws); birth rate → life expectancy (9%); electricity → births (6%); lower-secondary → births (5%); tax share → births (4%); gender parity in higher education → births (3%); birth rate → higher education (1%); birth rate → income (37%, not above the 400 draws). As before, these are group-specific stories, and the weakest are the ones resting on one region or one class.
- **No better than random (14):** birth rate → lower-secondary completion, → secondary completion (changed from group-specific), → electricity, → primary completion, → female labour participation, → tax share, → urbanisation; wage employment → birth rate; income → birth rate; farm employment → birth rate; female labour participation → birth rate; primary-school gender parity → birth rate; birth rate → child mortality; birth rate → wage employment. These are real in the pooled matrix but patchy across slices.
- **Falsifiers:** the fertility robust links are the two that survive the halves split and the t-fit version; the new/changed fertility verdicts are the most fragile claims here (they depend on the normal fit of the yardstick).

## (2) Massification
- **Nothing is robust everywhere** among the drivers, unchanged.
- **Beat random without a clear group (7):** life expectancy → gender parity in secondary (25%, above all 400 draws); secondary → lower-secondary completion (23%); tax share → gender parity in higher education (16%); higher education → secondary (14%); income → primary completion (12%, above all draws); female labour participation → secondary completion (11%, new); farm output → primary completion (10%, above all draws).
- **Hold only in particular kinds of country (30):** primary completion → gender parity in secondary (53% of groups; still grouped-specific, as before); child mortality → gender parity in higher education (47%); child mortality → higher education (43%, above all draws; the sign reverses with trends, so treat with care); child mortality → secondary (43%, above all draws); life expectancy → higher education (40%, above all draws); child mortality → primary completion (30%); farm output → lower-secondary (26%); electricity → lower-secondary (16%, above all draws) and → higher education (15%, above all draws); and 20 more schooling links concentrated in the low-birth-rate classes or single regions. The single-region and single-class claims remain the weakest evidence in the whole run.
- **Electricity:** once country trends are allowed it is zero in the whole sample, and the grouped evidence is patchy: 11 of its 32 links show more sign-flipping than random groupings (the most of any driver), while a cluster of its schooling links does beat random in specific classes. So electricity is a weak, group-dependent driver — the earlier conclusion stands and is sharper at 400 draws.
- **Paid jobs:** still **no** wage-employment link to a schooling outcome beats random groupings in any kind of group. This is the clearest null in the run.
- **Farm change:** farm output → higher education is now group-specific (was "beats random" in the 74-draw normal approximation) and is concentrated in low-birth-rate and low-child-mortality groups; farm output → lower-secondary holds only weakly; farm output → primary completion beats random. Farm employment → gender parity in higher education loses its "beats random" status.
- **Urbanisation and income → secondary completion:** both still no better than random (patchy in slices), unchanged.
- **Falsifiers:** the halves test (below) shows the main-tier set is stable to ±1 link; the electricity and single-region claims are the ones a larger run would most likely move.

## Tests designed to kill these findings
- **Determinism (repeat one draw):** replicates 7, 250 and 400 and the whole real-grouping pass were recomputed from scratch in a fresh process; all outputs are **bit-identical** (max difference 0, zero flip mismatches), 34-42 seconds each.
- **Exact reproduction of the 74-draw run:** the 74 overlapping draws are numerically identical (max difference 0 in the statistics and the heterogeneity tests), and the old real pass is identical. Run through the old analysis code path, the old classification counts reproduce exactly (159 no-better / 81 group-specific / 27 beats-random / 5 robust). This is what licenses reading every 400-draw difference as "better-estimated yardstick", not code drift.
- **Split the 400 draws into halves (first 200 vs last 200):** counts above all 200 draws agree in 30 links and disagree in 8; the smoothed-null significant set is 42 links in both halves (43 vs 49 individually, 8 disagreements, overlap 84%); full-400 significance agrees with a half in 267 and 269 of 272 links; the two halves' p-values rank-correlate at 0.994; their random-share means correlate at 0.9998 with a maximum difference of 0.76 percentage points. **400 draws is enough: the verdicts are stable at the link level.** The 74-draw yardstick was not (its random-share means differ from the 400-draw means by up to 0.89 points, and its p-values depend on the normal approximation).
- **Normal versus t fit of the yardstick:** the normal fit certifies 46 links, the t fit 42 (median fitted degrees of freedom 157, i.e. close to normal); they agree on 42 and disagree on 4. The t version keeps 40 of the old 43 (dies: 3) and adds 2. So the main-tier conclusion does not depend on normality; the 4 borderline links do.

## Nulls and things that are not robust
- 161 of 272 links are **no better than random** (was 159), and only 30 beat random in a way not explained by a specific group. The honest reading of the run is "a small set of strong links, a large null remainder".
- The **group-specific** labels are the least certain verdicts: they combine a smoothed-null test across ~16,600 tag-by-link comparisons with old "half the groups significant" thresholds. At 400 draws a single tag claim cannot be certified strictly (the same floor arithmetic applies), so the named groups are descriptions of where a link is concentrated, not certified claims. Single-region and single-class claims are the weakest.
- **Provisional:** the exact numbers 35/19/15/53 depend on BH's positive-dependence assumption for the shared 400 draws; the counts would be slightly smaller under a dependence-free (Bonferroni) rule and slightly larger under more draws.
- None of the 159 old "no better than random" links is above the 400-draw ceiling (and 18 of the 81 old group-specific links are), so the large null remainder is not a power artefact — those links really are indistinguishable from random groupings at this resolution.
