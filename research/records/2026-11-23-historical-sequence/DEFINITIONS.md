# Operational definitions, fixed before the dates were assembled (23 November 2026)

Committing this before the evidence table so that "schooling first" is not decided by choosing thresholds
after seeing the cases. If a case cannot meet these definitions, it is marked as such rather than stretched.

## The two events
**Schooling take-off.** The earlier of (a) the first *national* statute creating or compelling primary
schooling, where it was enforced (not merely proclaimed), and (b) the first year of a sustained rise in a
measured national primary enrolment or attainment series (a rise that is not reversed for at least 20 years).
Both dates are recorded separately because the gap between them is itself evidence about supply-led reform:
a statute that precedes the measured rise is a supply push; a measured rise that precedes the statute is
demand reveal.
**Farm exodus.** The first year of a sustained fall in the agricultural share of the labour force: either the
year the share begins a decline that is not reversed for at least 20 years, or the year it crosses 50 per cent,
whichever comes first and can be documented. Where only one of the two can be dated, the table says which.

## The verdict rule (fixed in advance)
- **Supply-led**: schooling take-off precedes the farm exodus by **10 years or more**, and the take-off is
  statute-driven (state creates the schools).
- **Demand-led**: the farm exodus precedes the schooling take-off by **10 years or more**, and the take-off
  follows measured demand (enrolment rises as industrial jobs pull families in).
- **Simultaneous**: the two dates are within 10 years of each other, or the evidence cannot order them.
Ten years is chosen because the median mortality-fertility lead in the project's own data is about 12 years,
so a shorter gap could not be distinguished from noise in annual series.

## Evidence classes
- **VERIFIED**: dated in a primary or near-primary source, or read from a series present on the box, with the
  file and column named.
- **UNVERIFIED**: a standard secondary date (textbook/literature consensus) that I have not checked against a
  primary source in this session; the report says so.
- **CONTESTED**: the literature disagrees; both dates are given.
Every row carries its class and what would settle it.

## Quantitative bonus
For at least two cases, the lead/lag is quantified as the difference between the schooling take-off and the
farm-exodus dates, with the underlying series named, interpolated gaps flagged, and the uncertainty of each
date reported as the width of the plausible window (e.g. a statute with enforcement lag, or a series with a
missing decade). No regression is used: with four cases, a regression would be decoration.
