## Executive summary — ranked by consequence

1. **Blocker:** Q3 uses effective fertility, not total fertility; its nine-country TFR benchmark must be withdrawn (F01).
2. **Blocker:** The sequencing draft still treats the 7% demand figure as substantive evidence despite unequal series starts (F02).
3. **Blocker:** Q2's two fertility outcomes measure different things; their disagreement is expected, not a data mystery (F03).
4. **Important:** 31/34 is a selective, threshold-sensitive comparison; the bounds across 64 span 48.4–95.3% (F04).
5. **Important:** Backcast skill assumes donor histories exist in the hidden period and uses a different naive baseline from the one described (F05).
6. **Important:** Q3 uses full-series information to select the holdout window; the double-logistic is unconstrained and not the UN model (F06).
7. **Important:** The multiverse's “robust everywhere” rule does not establish homogeneous effects; the BY conclusion needs a complete step-up calculation (F07).
8. **Important:** The minority-effect simulation reproduces 85/100, but its design was revised after inspecting results; do not claim blinded calibration (F08).
9. **Important:** Insignificant GDP and mortality coefficients do not establish no association; samples change across models (F09).
10. **Important:** The fertility audit cannot distinguish development trends, anticipation and schooling effects, and some draft language does not maintain that distinction (F11).

## Reproduction and checks

A clean copy of each record was run from its `results/` directory with isolated Python 3.10 and `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1`: NumPy 2.2.6, pandas 2.3.3, SciPy 1.15.3, statsmodels 0.15.0. Commands: `python3 -I ../scripts/<name>.py ../data` for `placebo_orthogonal`, `n4_gdp_mortality`, `demand_series_start_check`, `q1_threshold_sweep`, `q2_adult_stock_placebo`, `q3_form_race`, `q5_backcast_validation`; `python3 -I ../scripts/known_truth_sim.py 100 400`; `python3 -I ../scripts/summarise_sim.py > known_truth_sim_summary.md`. `diff -qr` between each original and rerun `results/` returned no differences other than the expected, uncommitted `q5_backcast.csv.gz`. `cmp` of the three first-record stdout logs, Q2 stdout, and `known_truth_sim_run3.log` returned 0. Thus the outputs generated in this run reproduce byte for byte; unchanged historical or discarded files were not independently regenerated. Exact reproducibility is environment-dependent and does not establish validity.

- Q1: 64/64 schooling and release dates agree with the committed input event table; reproduction gives 31/3/30, 18-year median (18 both-dated). These are **detected threshold dates**, with an asymmetric observation window. Its `classify` correctly treats release-censored/schooling-earlier cases as undetermined, but combines ties and reversals. Among the default 64, no observed equal school/release dates; 31 school onsets fall on their first available year, none with first year later than 1965. This check does not establish complete histories or exact historical events. Threshold sweeps change both eligible and identifiable samples; do a common-sample decomposition before attributing all variation to threshold alone.
- Same committed `n1_event_dates.csv`: `dropna` on three event columns and compare schooling vs fertility yields **24 / 20 / 20** for less/equal/greater; fertility vs farm release **60 / 0 / 4**. These are comparisons of recorded dates, not censoring-corrected true-order estimates.
- Q2: 1,417 observations / 109 countries for both level outcomes; 0 of those used rows have misdated ±10-year shifts, despite 19 irregular 5-year-grid gaps in the full panel. Country and year FE and country-clustered covariance are in the level model; changes model uses country FE only. Each schooling lag is separately standardized, so coefficients per SD are not directly per common unit. Sp20 is scaled before the four-term sample is restricted. Changes are overlapping and use EFR; country clustering does not solve confounding or anticipation.
- Simulation: AR(1) shocks and varying country slopes are generated as coded; country demeaning/detrending and cluster score sandwich implement the stated one-regressor slope up to its chosen finite-cluster correction. BH is a correct step-up algorithm for its 20 tag p-values; the `(1+exceedances)/(R+1)` floor is 1/401 at R=400. Validity still depends on exchangeable labels under the chosen null, not BH arithmetic. A single common permutation preserves cross-family dependence, and the simulated label families are correlated; it is a stylized null, not a full rerun of the original 272-link multiverse. The modified heterogeneity rule was tuned after observing A; post-look DGP changes also limit generalization. The 85/100 paper-rule result is unchanged by the subsequent heterogeneity split, but selection of this scenario can bias how representative that headline appears.
- Q3: BIC formula and parameter counts are coded as claimed, but RSS-based BIC treats serially dependent annual observations as independent. Eight randomly selected starts do not prove a global optimum, and unsuccessful optimizer status is not excluded. Holdout is last ten *rows*, not necessarily years. Selection criteria do not verify a plateau. All nine are a selected European sample; 6/9 is descriptive only.
- Q5: train/test countries are disjoint within each split, so donor histories are not target histories; this is not direct target leakage. The masked historical period is nevertheless available for donors, possibly unrealistic for intended applications. Three random repetitions are pooled for the reported RMSE rather than averaging their three separate RMSEs; calibration and evaluation countries are split by sorted ISO within pooled repeats, with repeated, correlated country-years. Coverage is empirical and not a finite-sample guarantee. Restriction to complete 1950–2020 histories selects especially well-covered countries. `1-RMSE/RMSE(M0)` is correctly implemented for the actual M0; it is not R².
- N4: the input file name is misleading, but code logs the levels. `near` uses at most two years and checks earlier before later at equal distance. HC1 is used. From rerun: schooling +0.164 (0.047) for onset and +0.270 (0.072) for midpoint, n=90; GDP -0.318 (0.583), n=68. These reproduce the stated figures, but model comparisons use different complete-case samples and post-hoc analyses cannot establish independence or null effects.

## What remains unverified and next steps

The original supply-demand wage input and full WDI data provenance are not established; the source script names an absolute `/root/work/data` path without a hash. The original multiverse country panel was not rerun and all four strict-tier p-value arrays were not checked; an observed partition is not automatically exchangeable with random partitions merely because sizes match, especially when group definitions are selected or correlated with data quality. BY controls FDR under arbitrary dependence **only when the individual null p-values are valid**; using it does not rescue an invalid permutation null. The methods paper's “0 / 35 / 44” is not a confidence interval, and 44 is an explicitly anti-conservative sensitivity. Separate four procedures do not automatically control the union of claims. Full paper-by-paper source and literature checks, the remaining Gemini numbers, and external data-source verification remain open. No script fixes were made; suggested repairs require substantive choices and independent tests.

The broad conclusion **no causal mechanism has been identified by the analyses examined** survives. It is not a finding that no causal mechanism exists. The proposed four papers are a provisional editorial plan, not a validated publication recommendation: sequencing and fertility audit need language and sampling repairs; the shape note needs a valid TFR benchmark; the methods note needs calibration and restrained verdicts. This partial review cannot certify all programme experiments or decide novelty.

**For Trey:** Trust the reproduced calculations as calculations, especially the censored sequencing counts and the distinction between levels and changes; do not treat them as proof of causation. Stop saying that the 7% result disproves labour demand, that effective fertility is total fertility, or that a multiverse label proves an effect travels everywhere. Next, (1) replace Q3's outcome with validated long-run TFR and repeat the model comparison with a genuine holdout, (2) rebuild the sequencing and demand comparisons on documented common observation windows, and (3) pre-specify and calibrate the multiverse verdicts on independent simulations before making claims about generality.
