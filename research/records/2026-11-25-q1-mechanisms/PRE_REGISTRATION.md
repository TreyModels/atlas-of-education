# Pre-registration — Q1: can genuinely different mechanisms fit the same aggregate history?
Committed before any data generation, fitting or GPU run. 25 November 2026. Branch
`research/q1-mechanisms-2026-11-25`.

This follows the Codex handoff (branch `research/codex-handoff-run-2026-11-24`, letter and SYNTHETIC.md): the
supplied `competing_worlds.py` is a unit check only, deliberately built so that two of its worlds are
observationally identical, with oracle-supplied rates and no fitting. This pilot replaces oracle scoring with
fitting, and adds a third world that is not a relabelled copy of the first two.

## The question, stated as three testable sub-questions
1. **Indistinguishability.** When the same aggregate historical history (country-cohort mean schooling and
   mean births, plus a development index) is generated by mechanisms that differ in their causal structure, can
   a fitting procedure tell the mechanisms apart from that history alone?
2. **Discrimination.** Which independently measured variables, added to the same training information and the
   same search budget, recover the true mechanism, and at what strength?
3. **Honesty.** Where recovery fails, is the failure a property of the world (observational equivalence) or of
   the pilot (small n, weak signal)? Report both, with Monte Carlo uncertainty.

## The three worlds (different kernels, explicit intervention semantics)
All three worlds generate the **same observables**: for country c and cohort t, mean years of schooling S_ct,
total births per woman F_ct, log income X_ct, mean age at first birth A_ct, and a micro sample of individuals
with (schooling, births, wealth quintile). What differs is the data-generating kernel.

**W1, direct schooling mechanism.** Births depend on schooling: F_i = exp(a_c + b_t - tau * S_i) + noise, with
schooling assigned by family background. **Intervention semantics:** an exogenous increase in schooling holding
the environment fixed reduces births by tau per unit (a direct effect). The schooling-birth association is
causal.

**W2, common-development selection.** A latent development level D_ct drives both schooling and births:
S_ct = s0 + s1 * D_ct + noise, F_ct = f0 - f1 * D_ct + noise, D_ct itself following income. There is **no direct
schooling effect**: tau = 0. **Intervention semantics:** an exogenous increase in schooling holding D fixed
changes no births; the observable gradient is selection. W2 is calibrated so that its conditional mean of F given
S matches W1's (the Codex construction, now fitted rather than oracle-supplied).

**W3, fertility-education joint choice.** Families choose both outcomes given a latent preference/price pi:
(S_i, F_i) are drawn from a bivariate normal with mean vector depending on income and a price shifter, and the
correlation rho. **Intervention semantics:** a change in the price of schooling moves schooling and births
jointly through the choice rule — a non-zero response that is *not* W1's direct dose effect and not W2's null.
Its signature is that the response to a price shift differs from the response to a schooling shift, and it
predicts a different age-at-first-birth profile (a tempo response).

## Training information (identical for all rivals)
A bounded, realistic panel: **24 countries x 8 five-year cohorts**, with country fixed effects declared in every
rival. The training block is country-cohort means of schooling, births, income and (where the measurement set
includes it) age at first birth. Training countries/cohorts are frozen by a seeded split before any fitting:
**16 training countries, 8 held-out countries; the last 2 cohorts of every country are held out.** The split
seed is recorded. No rival sees the held-out block during fitting.

## Rivals and comparable budgets
Three rivals, each a **different kernel**, fitted by maximum likelihood on exactly the same training arrays:
1. **R1 direct:** births on schooling + country and cohort fixed effects.
2. **R2 selection:** births on income + country/cohort fixed effects, with schooling modelled as an outcome of
   income (a two-equation system). No schooling coefficient in the births equation.
3. **R3 joint choice:** a bivariate model of (schooling, births) with a price/income shifter and correlation
   rho; births equations exclude a direct schooling term but include the price interaction.
Every rival gets the **same number of optimiser starts and evaluations** (recorded), the same convergence
tolerance and the same held-out scoring rule. A rival that fails to converge is reported as failed, not dropped.

## Evaluation, frozen before looking
Primary metrics, computed by a separate evaluation script that reads only the frozen results:
- **Mechanism recovery:** does the highest held-out predictive log score correctly identify the true world?
  Reported in a 3x3 confusion matrix over R = 40 replications per truth x strength cell, with Monte Carlo
  standard errors (binomial, sqrt(p(1-p)/R)).
- **Parameter recovery:** bias and RMSE of the mechanism-relevant parameter (W1's tau, W2's zero, W3's rho and
  price response) across replications.
- **Intervention recovery:** the predicted sign and magnitude of the world's own intervention contrast, for each
  rival, scored against the known truth.
- **Strength ladder:** three strengths (weak, medium, strong signal) so that "indistinguishable at weak
  strength, separable at strong" is a reportable outcome.
Measurement sets, evaluated in this order: (a) aggregate history only; (b) + micro schooling-births distribution;
(c) + age at first birth; (d) + wealth-gradient births. Each set adds data to the *evaluation*, never to the
training information.

## Falsifiers and negative controls stated in advance
- **Equivalence control:** a fourth, deliberately identical pair (W1 and a copy of W1 with a cosmetic relabel)
  must return chance-level recovery; if the machinery "recovers" a difference there, the machinery is broken and
  every other result is void.
- **Zero-signal control:** at strength zero (all mechanisms made identical) recovery must be ~33 per cent
  (chance among three). Failure here invalidates the ladder.
- **Seed independence:** data-generation seeds and fitting seeds are drawn from independent streams and both
  recorded; a replication on a second independent seed block must agree within Monte Carlo error.
- **No model winner is a cause:** whatever the recovery rates, the report states that a fitted model is not an
  established causal mechanism, and that the pilot is synthetic.
