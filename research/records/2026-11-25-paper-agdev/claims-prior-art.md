## The closest competitor papers

- **Replication:** Boppart, Kiernan, Krusell & Malmberg (2023). No other paper found in this search rebuilds their cross-sectional objects from public data. Our increment is the rebuild and the honest partial-replication verdict, not the mechanism.
- **Sequencing claim:** Squicciarini and Voigtländer (2015, *QJE*) and de Pleijt and van Zanden (2016, *EREH*) for the human-capital-before-industry direction; Sandberg (1979) for the canonical Swedish case; the world-polity school (Boli et al., 1985; Meyer et al., 1992; Ramirez & Boli, 1987) for the institutional account. Our increment is the 64-country systematic coding and the supply-demand horse race, **not** the direction.
- **Dynamic failure:** Alder, Boppart and Müller (2022) and Alvarez-Cuadrado, Long and Poschke (2017) are the closest dynamic/structural accounts; they impose or estimate sequencing through theory, while this paper reports that a country panel cannot identify it.

## Honesty rules applied

1. **No "first".** The paper claims a partial replication, a correction, a new test, and a new systematic measurement — never a first discovery. The direction of the sequencing result is attributed to Sandberg (1979) and the world-polity school.
2. **Real new-vs-replicated column** is in the table above: claims 1-5 replicated, 6-7 new tests, 8 correction, 9 new measurement, 10 new test/decomposition, 11 demonstration, 12 procedural.
3. **The earlier project test is described factually**: it measured farm employment's exposure to a common world price shock and contained none of BKKM's objects; that is a statement about what the test measured, not about anyone's competence.
4. **Unverifiable citations are marked.** Sandberg (1979), Kuznets (1957/1973), Timmer's book, and the two Foster-Rosenzweig entries could not be resolved in-session and are flagged in `refs.md`; they are cited only where the claim does not depend on their exact page numbers.
5. **The model is labelled a demonstration** wherever it appears; no counterfactual is presented as a forecast.
6. **A source field that counted ties is not cited.** The fertility-ordering figure in `sequencing.json` (`pct_schooling_before_fertility` = 68.75) counts the 20 simultaneous schooling-fertility take-offs as schooling-first. The paper reports the strict recomputation — 24/64 = 37.5% (95% CI 26.6-50.0%), 20 simultaneous (31.3%), 20 fertility-first (31.3%), median gap 0 (IQR −5 to +8); all 81 with both dates 29/81 = 35.8% — from `event_dates.csv` as recomputed in `research/paper-massification-2026-11-25` @ `95dc135`. The source record is annotated at `subsistence-v2` @ `8caaeac`. The weak schooling-fertility ordering is one reason the paper's claim rests on the schooling-before-release result, not on a full schooling → fertility → release sequence.
