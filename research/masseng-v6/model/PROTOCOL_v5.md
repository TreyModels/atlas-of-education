# Protocol v5: the demand engine

Written 2026-11-22, before any v5 parameter is fitted. It is not edited after the first v5 fit; amendments go at
the end, dated. v1–v4 and their protocol (`PROTOCOL.md`) stay unchanged and reproducible.

**Status of the test.** The post-1995 data have been seen in earlier versions, so the v5 test is
**retrospective and inspected**, not blind. What v5 can still show honestly:
- whether a theory-driven demand engine beats the v3 engine and the naive rules on the same held-out years;
- whether the separating observations favour one demand account over the others.

## 1. The question

What demand signal produced Philippine massification? From whom did it come, and did it become less sensitive
to cost and income as schooling became the norm?

## 2. What the model must reproduce (dependent variables)

- Enrolment ratios by level (GER, NER).
- Private shares.
- The rich–poor participation gradient.
- **New in v5:** household spending on education, as a share of family spending, nationally and by income group
  (FIES); and the household share of total education spending (PIDS National Transfer Accounts).
- Tertiary enrolment through the K-12 transition (CHED).

## 3. What drives it from outside (independent variables)

Held in `countries/<ISO>/drivers_v5.json`, each with its source:
- cohort sizes and official age and cycle rules (as before);
- national income and its distribution (as before);
- the wage premium for the next credential: college vs high school, and high school vs elementary;
- the share of employment that is wage and salary work (formal-economy proxy);
- dated policies (fees, subsidies, vouchers, free tuition, cash transfers, cycle changes).

Public senior high capacity and voucher places are supply and financing facts. They enter as inputs where
documented and are never fitted to enrolment.

## 4. The demand engine

The utility of continuing into or within level L, for income decile d in year y:

    alpha_L                                   level intercept (entry intercepts separate)
  + b_h * premium_L(y)                        human-capital value: wage premium of completing L
  + b_p * S_L(y-1)                            positional/relational pull: share of 20-29 year-olds holding
                                              L's credential, generated by the model (the steam engine)
  + b_f * formal(y)                           relational floor: queues that require credentials
  + eta_x * ln(decile income / mean)          cross-sectional income (found to be zero in v3)
  - kappa * exp(-lambda * S_L(y-1)) * ln(1 + cost_L,s,d(y) / income_d(y))
                                              affordability; lambda > 0 means cost sensitivity falls as
                                              the credential becomes the norm

National income no longer enters directly. It works only through decile incomes (affordability) and through the
drivers. Costs are direct costs, from a relative schedule scaled by `c_scale`, plus declared forgone earnings.
Direct costs produce the household spending that the model must match.

**Government** acts in three ways, kept separate so no policy is counted twice:
- it sets the required cycle (structure, including K-12);
- it pays (cost changes, vouchers, cash transfers);
- it builds (public capacity).

**Deferred entry.** Secondary completers who do not enter tertiary straight away stay eligible until age 25.
Each year a declared share `h_def` reconsiders with the same utility.

## 5. Rivals (all fitted on the same training data)

| Rival | Demand terms |
|---|---|
| R0 | v3 engine: `eta * ln(national income)` + `eta_x` + cost. The benchmark structure. |
| R1 | Full v5: `b_h`, `b_p`, `b_f`, `lambda`, `eta_x`, cost. |
| R2 | Human capital only: `b_p = b_f = lambda = 0`. |
| R3 | R1 with `lambda = 0`: constant cost sensitivity. |
| R4 | R1 with `b_p = 0`: no positional feedback. |

## 6. Separating observations, written before fitting

1. **2013–2024.** The college premium falls (0.88 to 0.59) while tertiary GER rises. R2 predicts flat or falling
   participation; R1 and R4 predict a continued rise.
2. **Recessions** (1984–86, 1998). R3 and R2 predict comparable dips at secondary and tertiary level. R1 predicts
   a smaller secondary dip if `lambda > 0`, because secondary was nearer saturation.
3. **The K-12 recovery** (2019–2024), with deferred entry, against CHED headcounts.
4. **FIES 2012:** education spending share by income class (0.4% to 5.4%).
5. **Household share of total education spending**, PIDS NTA 1991–2010.

## 7. Training and test

- **Training:**
  - enrolment series 1971–1995 (GER for three levels, primary NER, primary private share);
  - tertiary private share 1985;
  - APIS 2019 richest/poorest ratios (calibration since v3);
  - FIES education share, national and bottom 30% vs upper 70%, 2003 and 2006 (pins `c_scale`).
- **Test:**
  - enrolment 1996–2024;
  - FIES 2012 by income class;
  - FIES 2023 national;
  - NTA household share;
  - CHED headcounts 2016–2020;
  - private shares as in v3.
- **Benchmarks:** last value carried forward, straight line, and the v3 model refitted on the same training data.
- **Decision rules:**
  - The preferred rival has the lowest mean test RMSE on the three GERs.
  - It is called supported on a separating observation only if it beats every rival on that observation.
  - `lambda > 0` is supported only if R1 beats R3 on observation 2 and on test RMSE.
- **Recovery test** on the preferred rival, 20 replicates. Pass: `b_h`, `b_p` and `kappa` each recovered within
  ±25% in at least 80% of replicates. If it fails, the status stays `not identified` for the failing parameters.

## 8. Port

Cambodia runs the same engine. Its own drivers and targets are fitted on 1995–2010 and tested on 2011–2024. The
DHS 2010 quintile attendance is a training target. The 2014 Bac II crackdown is the separating observation for
the exam gate: tertiary entry should fall and rerouting should rise.

## Additions made before any v5 fit (2026-11-22, 01:25 local)

These came from reviewing ChatGPT's comments on the working paper. No v5 parameter had been fitted, so they are
part of the registered design, not amendments.

**A credential threshold driver, R_L(y).**
- **What it is:** the share of employment in occupations that normally require L's credential. The proxy is
  managers, professionals, technicians and clerical workers as a share of total employment (LFS/ILOSTAT).
- **Dated rules:** civil service qualification standards enter as dated steps.
- **When data are missing:** `formal(y)` is used in its place, and the report says so.
- **How it enters utility:** as `b_r * R_L(y)`.
- **Rival R5:** cost sensitivity falls with the threshold rather than with saturation:
  `kappa * exp(-lambda * R_L)` instead of `exp(-lambda * S_L)`.

**Feedback direction.** Massification raising employers' requirements, and requirements raising massification,
are both possible. The model represents R_L as exogenous. The report must say that the direction cannot be
identified from aggregate timing alone. It must also say what dated evidence would separate the two directions:
changes in occupational requirements measured before the enrolment changes.

**Additional separating observations.**

6. **Grade 10 to Grade 11 transition in SY2016-17 (DepEd: 93%).** A cost-only account predicts a sharp fall
   when two years are added; threshold and positional accounts predict high progression.
7. **Cambodia 2014 Bac II.** Of those who failed, more rerouted to associate and technical programmes than
   resat or left. Credential demand survives a blocked path if rerouting is large.

**Measurement discipline.** Three distinctions are kept separate in every reported result:
- a falling wage premium is not falling absolute earnings;
- aggregate household spending is not spending per student;
- financing shares (NTA, the World Bank figures for Cambodia) are not per-pupil costs.
