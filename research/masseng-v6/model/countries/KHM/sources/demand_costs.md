# Cambodia demand, costs, returns, targets (compiled 2026-11-22)

All figures were read through a summarising fetch tool, not from raw PDFs, unless noted. Machine-readable twin: demand_costs.json. "NOT FOUND" means searched and absent. Do not edit facts.md (earlier file); items already there are only cross-referenced.

By domain (2014 / 2019-20): Cambodia 759/1,266; Phnom Penh 1,643/3,132; other urban 1,179/1,312; other rural 578/821. Education was about 2.1% of household consumption in 2019/20 (Executive Summary). Reliability: high (official), though the data are nominal riel. Quintile breakdown of this spending: NOT FOUND.

**World Bank 2005, "Cambodia: Quality Basic Education for All"** (Report 32619-KH, Jan 2005; https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/b747a216-e865-5bae-837e-caf3f2aad33c/content). Data from Bray & Seng 2004 private-cost study (77 primary schools resurveyed from 1997/98, 39 lower secondary), exchange rate US$1 = 3,835 riel.
- Table 18, primary, riel per pupil per year: urban grade 1 total 116,900, grade 6 214,200; rural grade 1 42,500, grade 6 107,200. Tutoring: urban g1 21,000, urban g6 48,700, rural g6 3,300 (rural g1-5 zero).
- Table 19, lower secondary: urban g7 379,300, g8 402,700, g9 559,500 (tutoring g9 211,400); rural g7 191,300, g8 200,600, g9 275,200 (tutoring g9 63,500).
- Rural cost as % of 1999 poverty line: g1 5.3, g6 14.2, g7 25.5, g9 37.8.
- 1997 SES: schooling spending per pupil took 26.2% of non-food spending for the poorest vs 11.9% for the richest (primary); 56.2% vs 26.2% (secondary). The extract's wording on which group each number belongs to is ambiguous; verify before use.
- Household share of primary resources 76.9% (1997/98) falling to 55.6% (2004); lower secondary households 65.9% (2004). Parental share of total education expenditure 37% to 34% (2002).
- Grade 9 direct costs exceed 1,000,000 riel (about US$250) per year, mostly tutoring and opportunity cost (Section 4.5).
- Reliability: good for relative patterns. PAP 2000 reduced fees but costs per pupil remained large.

**Bray (1999) and Bray & Bunly (2005)**: The original 1999 report, *The Private Costs of Public Schooling* (IIEP/UNESCO in collaboration with UNICEF, 1997/98 data), was checked through UNESCO's full-text record on 2026-11-22: https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000117632 . It directly confirms, among other figures, Takeo direct school expenses as 11.9%, 8.6%, 7.2%, 5.7%, 10.0% and 20.4% of estimated family income for Grades 1–6 respectively; the separate Phnom Penh column must not be mixed into that sequence. It also confirms rural/urban education spending by quintile (1996): poorest 4.1%/4.8%, second 3.7%/5.4%, middle 3.6%/5.5%, fourth 4.0%/5.0%, richest 3.6%/5.9%, total 3.8%/5.3%. These are sample-specific household estimates, not national administrative accounts. Bray & Bunly 2005, *Balancing the Books* (CERC Monograph 4, https://cerc.edu.hku.hk/?p=1181), separately reports the 1999 book's estimate that households and communities met 59.0% of total primary-school resources; keep households+communities distinct from households alone. Secondary citation (Brehm & Silova, https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/10087648/1/Brehm_All%20education%20for%20some.%20International%20development%20and%20shadow%20education%20in%20Cambodia_AAM.pdf): tutoring was 1-19% of household education spending across quintiles (1997/8 national survey, Bray & Bunly p.53); NEP 2007 survey (210 households, 4 provinces, 2006): tutoring 78% of household basic-education cost; Brehm et al. 2012 (444 students, 6 schools, one province): regular tutoring 68.4% in grade 9, 41.3% in grade 6. Bray & Kwo (2013) was read (https://hub.hku.hk/bitstream/10722/189483/1/Content.pdf?accept=1): contains NO Cambodia spending numbers. National tutoring prevalence: NOT FOUND (facts.md has the Siem Reap 81.9% sample).

**Tertiary household cost (CSES 2019, via World Bank 2024)**: median annual cost bottom wealth quintile KHR 4,165,000; top quintile KHR 6,856,000 (gap 65%). Household tertiary expenditure up 47% real 2010-2019; individual annual cost up 62%. https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/bitstreams/b9d36ecb-f91b-49b3-ad34-c09e1043e1a7/download (Section 5.2.2). Reliability: good.

## 5. Tertiary and Bac II
- Public/private HE students: 2000-01 7,291 / 18,125; 2004-05 7,953 / 38,882 (ESP 2006-10 Table 1; "private supported" label). 2008-09: 137,000 total, 46,395 public (ESP 2009-13). World Bank 2024 (Figure 2.7 and Table 1.2): 183,000 (2015), 179,000 (2019), 176,000 (2022); 2022: public 62,991 (35.8%), private 112,971 (64.2%), total 175,962; public fell about 25,000 and private rose about 18,000 over 2015-2022. Phnom Penh Post cites CDRI as about 117,000 (2006-07) to over 245,000 (2011-12), and 56,301 first-year starters in 2015. These series conflict (e.g. 255k in 2012/13 vs 183k in 2015) due to definitions or double counting; do not splice. GER: 11.6% (2017-18), 13.3% (2019-20), 12.43% (2020-21) (ESP 2019-23; Education Congress 2021-22 report).
- Bac II candidates: 2014 registered 91,373 and 2015 registered 88,488, a 3% fall (https://phnompenhpost.com/national/thousands-drop-out-national-exams, 26 Jun 2015); 2015 sat 83,341 (facts.md). 2017 103,112 and 2018 117,062 (Phnom Penh Post 2018-08-21 via pressreader; year inferred from URL date, grade level not stated). 2021 114,180 registered (ODC); 2023 137,412; 2025 sat 144,848 and passed 122,473 (84.5%); 2026 151,238 (The Star). 2024 passed 106,351. NOT FOUND: 2016, 2019, 2022, and pre-2014 counts.
- After the 2014 reform, of those who failed: 11,686 decided to repeat senior year but only 4,695 registered for 2015; 15,635 went to associate degrees; 17,586 to technical training (same PPP article). The 2014 first-sitting pass rate was about 25% and about 37% after retakes (Phnom Penh Post, https://www.phnompenhpost.com/post-weekend/degrees-difficulty-cost-cheating). No tertiary-enrolment before/after estimate found. The WB 2024 report notes enrolment declining 2015-2022 but does not attribute it to the reform.
