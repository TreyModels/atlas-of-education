# Fact check

Checked on 2026-11-14 by web search and page fetch. Every figure has a URL and a status:
**read in source** (the page was opened and the figure appeared there; pages were read through a
fetch tool that returns a summary of the page, so wording is paraphrased) or **from search
snippet only** (nothing below is in that class: the search results returned titles only).
"Not found" means I looked and could not find it. Nothing is guessed.

**Main doubt about the headline number.** The 48% for Chinese women comes from an online opt-in panel
of 1,047 Chinese respondents, more educated than China as a whole, and the cell it is based on
(unmarried women 18-34) is smaller still and unreported. The chart marks the China gap as significant
at 95%, but the sample is not random, so that significance covers sampling noise only, not who
joins an online panel. I have not seen the question wording, so I do not know whether "undecided"
people were counted as not wanting to marry.

## 3. Sex ratio at birth for the cohorts aged 18-34 in 2026 (born 1992-2008)

Male births per 100 female births, UN World Population Prospects 2024 (28th edition) as published by

| Japan | 105.8 | 105.3 | 105.6 | 105.3 | 105.4 | 105.4 | 105.3-105.8 |

The full yearly values are in `data_inputs.py`; the model uses the 1992-2008 mean of each country.

Cautions:
- WPP China values are modelled and smooth. National sources report some years higher (a search result
  mentioned about 121 for one year, which I did not open, so it is **from search snippet only** and
  not used). The sweep therefore runs r up to 1.20.
- Birth ratios are not the ratios of unmarried 18-34 year olds in 2026: deaths, migration (including
  men going abroad and foreign brides), and the marriage pairing offset all matter. I did not look these up.
- A cross-check from national statistics for Korea (Statistics Korea) was found only through a journal
  article (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12006057/, read in source), which gives, calculated
  from its Table 1, 113.6 for 1992 and 108.2 for 2004 (WPP: 114.1 and 110.4). So the two Korean sources
  agree to within about 2 per 100 in these years but not exactly. Japan and China were not cross-checked
  against national statistics. **Not found:** a Japanese or Chinese national series.

## 5. Summary of what is and is not confirmed

- Confirmed: the survey exists, is online and non-probability, China n = 1,047, an educated sample.
- Not confirmed: the 48/77 itself, the chart page, the question wording, the unmarried 18-34 cell sizes.
- Other surveys agree on direction in two of three cases, but with different numbers and wording.
- Sex ratios at birth are confirmed for 1992-2008 from one source family (UN WPP 2024 via OWID) and partly cross-checked for Korea.
