# Quantitative estimates: rainfed lowland rice yield vs. drought/monsoon indices in mainland SE Asia

## Headline finding (state plainly)

**No published estimate exists of rice-yield change per unit PDSI (or scPDSI) for mainland Southeast Asia.** I searched for it in several ways (index-name queries, "per unit PDSI", rice/paddy × PDSI × each country, panel/elasticity phrasings) and found nothing. The only PDSI–rice analyses I located are for **Bangladesh** (Aus rice; PDSI vs *area*, not yield) and **Indonesia** (district-level GWR panel). Nothing per-PDSI for Thailand/Laos/Cambodia/Vietnam/Myanmar rice.

Equally, **no paper I could verify reports a standardized "x % yield per 1 SD of wet-season rainfall" elasticity for rainfed lowland rice in the region.** What exists is: (a) SPEI-1 **correlations/R²** per province (NE Thailand), (b) **rainfall semi-elasticities** in plot- or province-level production functions, (c) **crop-model % yield losses**, and (d) **qualitative** paleo links between reconstructed PDSI droughts and Angkorian agriculture.

Everything below is labelled ✓ = I read the text (full text or the open-access version of the same chapter) / ~ = abstract or indexed excerpt only.

## 4. On converting PDSI units to % yield

- **No such conversion exists (verified: none found)** for rice anywhere in mainland SE Asia. I found no paper that regresses rice yield on PDSI, scPDSI, or a reconstructed PDSI series in this region.
- PDSI is a dimensionless, soil/region-calibrated water-balance index (standard classes: −1 to −2 mild drought … ≤−4 extreme). Because the same PDSI value corresponds to very different mm-deficits across the coarse sandy soils of NE Thailand, the Mekong Delta clays and the Cambodian floodplain, a single PDSI→% yield conversion is not defensible even in principle.
- The nearest empirical anchors that *could* be used to build a bridge, with explicit caveats:
  - Khondakar et al. (2022): PDSI affects Aus-rice **area** strongly but yield weakly (Bangladesh) — warns that yield-based conversions will be biased toward zero.
  - Prabnakorn et al. (2018): SPEI-1 explains only **10–16 %** of yield variance — so any index→yield conversion for NE Thailand has a small signal-to-noise ratio.
  - Buckley et al. (2010): the reconstructed PDSI values (−7.20 at the 1403 extreme) are far outside the modern instrumental range, so calibrating a modern-index elasticity onto them would be extrapolation.

## 5. Practical summary of the numbers you can actually cite

- **NE Thailand, SPEI-1:** significant province-level relation, **R² = 0.10–0.16**; trend-attributable losses **<50 kg/ha/decade ≈ 3 % of mean yield** (Prabnakorn et al. 2018).
- **NE Thailand, rainfall:** stage-1 rainfall semi-elasticity **+0.079 per cm (n.s.)** (Felkner et al. 2009); 30-yr precipitation *normals* have **no significant** yield effect (Chankrajang & Talerngsri-Teerasuwannajak 2022).
- **Thailand, climate projections (rainfed/in-season):** **−23.7 % (RCP4.5) / −34.0 % (RCP8.5)** by 2046–55 (Pipitpukdee & Attavanich 2021); panel-model range **−4.6 to −33.8 %** (Sinnarong et al. 2019).
- **Laos, water stress:** **4–12 %** yield reduction from water stress alone; **3–10 %** from late-season drought by toposequence (Inthavong et al. 2014).
- **Vietnam Mekong (DSSAT):** **−5.5 to −8.5 %/yr** mid-century without CO₂ fertilization (Kontgis et al. 2019).
- **Cambodia (coupled model):** no significant regional drought–yield link detectable 2000–2016 (Abhishek et al. 2021).
- **Palaeo:** reconstructed March–May PDSI min −7.20 (1403); Angkor droughts 14th–15th c.; rice impact asserted, not quantified (Buckley et al. 2010; Lieberman & Buckley 2012; Buckley et al. 2014).

If the intended use is an Angkor/14th-century rice-yield-per-PDSI sensitivity, the honest answer is that **it does not exist in the published literature** and would have to be constructed (e.g. by combining Prabnakorn's SPEI-1 R² with a local PDSI–SPEI rescaling, or by running ORYZA/DSSAT with reconstructed PDSI-derived rainfall) — with the caveats in §4.
