# REVIEW — BRAY composite (drawing on Bray & Lykins 2012; Bray's comparative work; Bray & Zhang) — brief_006

**G. GROUNDING.** Literature basis: shadow education is stage-specific and gate-anchored; cross-national rates are
survey estimates with different denominators; the strongest comparative evidence is that participation is high at
upper-secondary gates in East Asia and teacher-sold in Cambodia/Philippines at lower-secondary/primary. **COUNCIL
EXTRAPOLATION:** the adjudication below is the composite's.

**M. MODEL RENDERING.** For an incidence claim the model must reproduce take-up by stage × provider × country *after*
fixing structural parameters independently of those rates. M1 stage take-up; M2 provider mix; M3 ranges predeclared;
M4 checks reported; M5 the calibration moment excluded from the check. Loop-3 S1 supplies M1–M5 and fails.

**A. VERDICT.** **Stop-rule event confirmed; incidence claim withdrawn.** The check is fair: 11 rows, stage-matched,
ranges declared, one calibration cell excluded, KOR/JPN honestly marked UNABLE. The observed pattern is worse than a
simple miss: loop 2 over-universalised (0.88–0.89 everywhere), loop 3 under-predicts most rows (PHL 23.5 vs 46.5;
KHM G9 21.3 vs 68.4, primary 12.8 vs 41.3; HKG lower 30.6 vs 72, middle 40.2 vs 82) while over-predicting PHL upper
(39.4 vs 28.1). A model that misses in both directions across stages cannot be tuned into the literature: it is not
representing the stage/provider structure. The one "match" the programme ever had (HK 0.85) was the fitted moment and
is correctly excluded.

**B. WHAT MAY SURVIVE.** Not incidence. What may survive: (i) the *comparative measurement* work — country cards and
the 21-row target register are sourced, dated, with NOT-FOUND flags; (ii) the *provider structure* as a structural
possibility (teacher-tutors dominate supply in this model class and in the comparative record) but with no level or
country validation; (iii) the *prior-work attribution*: the gate-position story belongs to Yoo (2003), and the
comparative record (not this model) is the place to make it.

**C. ESTIMAND.** "Incidence" is a share of students at a stage; the model's `take_S`/`take_C` are not the same
object unless stage-matched, and S1 does that. The failure is therefore about content, not definition.

**D. TYPE.** Any statement that the model "shows shadow education appears where the literature says" is a prediction,
not a finding; it is now falsified within the model class. Report as a failed check, not as a null result about the
world.

**E. STRONGEST DEFENSIBLE SENTENCE.** "Under the predeclared stage-matched check, the structural shadow model misses
the literature's take-up in all three assessable countries, in both directions across stages; the literature's
comparative pattern is not reproduced, and no shadow-education incidence claim is made."

**F. NEGATIVE.** The KHM gap (G9 21.3 vs 68.4) and PHL all-basic (23.5 vs 46.5) are large enough that the model would
need a different *structure* (teacher-sold supply, school-quality gradient), not a parameter, to close them — which is
itself informative about what the model lacks.

**G2. STEEL-MAN.** The strongest defence of the runner: the data are thin (KHM is a two-school case; PHL one select
survey; HKG second-hand). A referee may say the targets are soft, not the model wrong. But soft targets cannot
validate an incidence claim either — so the withdrawal stands; the honest product is the measurement audit.

**H. ALTERNATIVE.** Tutoring may be driven by school quality and social competition more than by gate stakes; the
model's null positional response is consistent with that, but the model has no school-quality channel to test it.

**I. WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND.** Independent nationally representative stage × provider series for ≥2 countries, and
a model that reproduces them without fitting take-up (the KOR no-fit benchmark is the only cheap candidate).
