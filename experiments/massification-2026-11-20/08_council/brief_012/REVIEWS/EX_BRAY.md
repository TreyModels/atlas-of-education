# REVIEW — BRAY composite (drawing on Bray & Lykins 2012; Bray & Kwok 2003; Bray's comparative work) — brief_012

**G. GROUNDING.** Literature basis: shadow-education spending is provider- and subject-specific; the KEDI survey
reports provider spend for **general subjects** (hagwon 241, 1:1 37, group 22, internet 16, textbook 11 — thousand won,
all levels) while total private-education spend (434k won/student; 462/596/740 per participant by level) includes
**arts/physical and materials**. **COUNCIL EXTRAPOLATION:** the composition assessment is the composite's.

**A. IDENTITY.** The arithmetic is internally consistent: participation (86.0/75.4/66.4) × spend per participant
(462/596/740k) reproduces the reported per-student series (397/449/491k) within 0.2%, and the log decomposition is an
exact identity (residual 0 by construction). No double counting of non-participants: the numerator is per-participant
spend; the per-student series is the participation-weighted product. That part is sound.

**B. THE COMPOSITION OBJECTION.** +79% "price/hour" is not a price. The numerator is an all-in private-education
basket; the denominator is survey participation hours. Between elementary and high school the basket almost certainly
changes: general-subject share rises, exam-prep (purpose: prep for next level 14.2% overall, likely higher at
high school) rises, provider mix shifts (hagwon/1:1 vs group), materials/arts shares change, and high-school samples
include vocational tracks. A rising per-hour residual is exactly what a basket upgrade produces. The KEDI tables on
disk give provider mix **only for all levels combined** — so the decomposition cannot separate price from basket.
Report "+79% spend per participating student-hour", not "price".

**C. WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT.** KEDI provider × level tables (Tables 9–11) and purpose × level; if within-provider,
within-subject spend/hour is flat while basket shifts, composition dominates; if it rises similarly, escalation is
supported. The Korean official survey publishes provider-type tables and income bands — a table/microdata request,
not new fieldwork.

**D. VERDICT.** T1 supports the *direction* of the two-margin rewrite (participation and intensity diverge) but not
the word "price". The paper's one-margin participation story is contradicted; the price-escalation interpretation is
unidentified until composition is decomposed.
