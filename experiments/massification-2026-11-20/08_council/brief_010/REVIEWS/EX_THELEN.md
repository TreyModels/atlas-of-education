# REVIEW — THELEN composite (drawing on Thelen 2004; Varieties of Capitalism) — brief_010

**G. GROUNDING.** Literature basis: institutional configurations are complementary bundles; "differentiation" is not
a count of tracks but a configuration of training, certification and employer recognition; "responsiveness" conflates
capacity with political willingness. **COUNCIL EXTRAPOLATION:** the institutional assessment is the composite's.

**A. THE TWO DIMENSIONS ARE UNDERSPECIFIED.** *Differentiation* as used in the paper (multiple valued pathways) is an
institutional configuration, not a one-dimensional score: Germany's dual system is not "differentiated" because it has
tracks but because employers, unions and the state co-produce certification and returns (Thelen 2004). The Netherlands'
HAVO/VWO/VMBO is a tracked system with mobility that depends on admissions rules and labour-market recognition. The
paper asserts "equivalent social legitimacy" for vocational routes — that is an empirical claim needing cohort flows,
returns by track, and employer recognition data, not a case narrative. *Responsiveness* similarly bundles fiscal
capacity, administrative capacity and political coalitions; the scout's capacity-binding flag (tax/GDP, GDP pc, 1990
GER, urban) is a reasonable proxy for the fiscal/administrative side, but it should be labelled as such and paired
with a **policy-change** measure (reform events per decade), otherwise "responsive" means "rich".

**B. THE KOREA CELL.** Korea does have extensive vocational options and expanded higher education; the paper's own
claim is that "differentiation without status recalibration" fails. That is a substantive institutional claim — it
requires showing the status hierarchy of institutions and the returns attached to tracks; the paper gives the SKY
anecdote and a regulator episode, not the institutional evidence. The HKG omission is the bigger problem: HKG's 2003
gradient (35.1/46.6/70.3) is the cleanest stage-rising case in this programme's data and belongs in Regime II
(with the second-hand caveat). A typology that puts Korea in II and omits HKG, the case that actually fits the
mechanism, is a warning sign.

**C. CELL COMPRESSION.** The 2×2 will compress heterogeneous systems: Singapore and Germany are not one regime
(different state–employer configurations, different tutoring markets), and India/Brazil are not one (different
informality and provider structures). The paper needs **within-cell variation** and explicit borderline cases, or the
typology becomes a labelling exercise.

**D. MEASUREMENT PROPOSAL.** Per country: vocational/technical share of upper-secondary enrolment; number of
nationally certified post-secondary pathways; track-mobility rate; returns to each pathway (if available); employer
credential requirement (brief 009 P17); capacity-binding flag; reform-event count. Four of these are constructible
from UIS/WDI/national sources; returns and employer requirements need the data acquisitions in brief 008 §4.

**E. VERDICT.** The typology is a plausible heuristic but currently a labelling scheme with no measures. It can be
salvaged as a **measurement agenda** (which is also the programme's strongest publishable object), not as a validated
classification.
