# REVIEW — BRAY composite (drawing on Bray & Lykins 2012; Bray & Kwok 2003; Bray's comparative work) — brief_010

**G. GROUNDING.** Literature basis: shadow education is stage- and provider-specific; the comparative record is rich
but uneven; Korea's official survey and Hong Kong's 2003 school survey are the best-measured cases; Cambodia's is a
case study, not a national rate. **COUNCIL EXTRAPOLATION:** the audit below is the composite's.

**A. REGIME AUDIT.** Regime IV (Cambodia teacher-sold) is the paper's strongest empirical section: G9 68.4% (193/282),
primary 41.3% (67/162), urban 60.5/rural 26.4, fees 500–1,000 Riel/session, teacher salary US$44/month vs US$80–100
with tutoring (`04_data/shadow_round2/SHADOW_DATA.md` §5). But it is **one district, six schools, 444 students** — the
paper must say so in the same paragraph, not in a footnote. Regime II (Korea): the on-disk official 2023 numbers are
78.5% all levels and **86.0 / 75.4 / 66.4** by level — v1's "over 75%" is fine, v2's "over 80%" is wrong except for
elementary; more damaging, participation *falls* with stage while Hong Kong 2003 *rises* (35.1/46.6/70.3). A
terminal-gate arms-race story fits HKG and not Korea as written; the missing structure is the two-margin fact
(participation down, spend/student +23.4% from 398 to 491k won). Regime I (Singapore/Finland/Netherlands) has **no
verified shadow-incidence data in this programme** — it is the thinnest cell and must be flagged as untested.
Regime III (India/Brazil) rests on citations I cannot verify here (Gupta 2021, Agarwal 2020, Whitaker 2010) and on a
misread of Banerjee et al. 2007 (a remedial-education RCT with positive effects, not a "minimal gains from private
tutoring" study).

**B. PROVIDER AND RITUAL.** The paper's provider descriptions are plausible but mostly unsourced for the countries it
names; the two provider facts on disk are KOR hagwon spend dominance (241/434k won) and HKG mass-lecture dominance at
S6-7 (72.4%). The ritual claim meets motive data: KOR purposes are instrumentally academic (makeup 49.6%, advance
24.0%, next-level 14.2%) and HKG is exam-preparation-dominated at the top (84.5%) with homework guidance at the
bottom (60.3%) — these are not disconfirmation, but they are not support either; the paper needs normative/motive
measures, not inference from persistence.

**C. VERDICT.** The framework is a genuinely interesting hypothesis, but the evidence table is currently
case-study-plus-assertion. Lead with Cambodia; use Korea/HKG as the anomaly that forces the two-margin model; delete
or source every Regime I sentence; and cite this programme's measurement limits (briefs 006/008) so the paper does
not imply a validation it does not have.
