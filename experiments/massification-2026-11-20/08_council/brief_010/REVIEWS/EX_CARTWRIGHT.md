# REVIEW — CARTWRIGHT composite (drawing on Cartwright 1999; Cartwright & Hardie 2012) — brief_010

**G. GROUNDING.** Literature basis: a capacity claim needs scope conditions and a test; a functional explanation is a
hypothesis about consequences, not evidence of intent; if a theory survives every outcome it is not a theory yet.
**COUNCIL EXTRAPOLATION:** the assessment below is the composite's.

**A. THE FALSIFIABILITY PROBLEM.** As written, "overflow governance" explains persistence (ritual), policy failure
(adaptive capacity), and transition (status recalibration) — a structure that cannot fail. Three separate claims are
being run together and need separating: (i) a **structural claim** (massification creates selection pressure that
shifts into para-institutions); (ii) a **functional claim** (this displacement stabilises the system); (iii) a
**ritual claim** (participation is legitimation). (i) is testable with measures; (ii) requires showing the
displacement actually stabilises (counterfactual: systems without it would delegitimise); (iii) requires motive/norm
data. The paper currently asserts (iii) from persistence — the weakest inference in the manuscript.

**B. "STATES DISPLACE" IS FUNCTIONALIST SLIDE.** Shadow education is not a state instrument; it is a household and
provider outcome that states may tolerate. The paper needs the intentional/unintended distinction: if the argument is
that displacement is an *unintended consequence*, the mechanism must be stated without agency; if officials
deliberately used para-institutions, document it (China 2021, Korea curfews, PH K-12). The programme's PH reform record
shows a state raising a floor, not designing overflow — a counterexample the paper should confront.

**C. OPERATIONALISATION.** Differentiation needs observable measures: vocational share of upper-secondary enrolment,
number of legitimate post-secondary pathways, track permeability, returns to non-university qualifications (brief 009
P17 is the employer-side measure). Responsiveness should collapse to the scout's single capacity-binding flag
(tax/GDP, GDP pc, 1990 secondary GER, urban share) plus a spending-response term (R01 = 0.111; Asia 0.189), not four
elasticities. Ritual needs time-use (KOR 7.3 h/week), motive surveys (KOR purposes; HKG 84.5% exam prep at S6-7 vs
60.3% homework guidance at S1-3) and norms — and on those data the ritual claim is not supported in the two cases we
can measure.

**D. THE FOUR PREDICTIONS.** The paper should predeclare: (1) bans re-route spend at unchanged gate stakes; (2)
transition requires status recalibration, regulation alone displaces; (3) Regime I is misclassified if tutoring is
required to clear gates; (4) ritual persists where marginal learning returns ≈0 and motives are normative rather than
exam-instrumental. Each needs a threshold; without thresholds they are slogans. On current evidence, (4) is at risk in
Korea and HKG.

**E. VERDICT.** The idea can become a real theory, but only after the structural/functional/ritual claims are
separated, the measures specified, and the predictions made refutable. As a submitted manuscript it would be rejected
for unfalsifiability and thin evidence — not for lack of originality.
