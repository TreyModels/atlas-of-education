## Disagreements preserved

**D1. BRAY vs PATRINOS — how strong is Regime IV?**
BRAY: it is the paper's only empirically anchored section and should lead, but as a case study. PATRINOS: it is a
six-school district study and cannot carry a regime claim on its own; the paper needs at least one measured second
case. *Resolution:* lead with it as a case study; do not promote it to a regime-wide claim; HKG 2003 can be the second
case only with its caveat.

**D2. PATRINOS vs THELEN — which paper is first?**
PATRINOS: the measurement paper is the only object with real data (KOR/HKG/KHM/PHL cards; shadow register;
parameter map); it should lead. THELEN: a "measurement paper" presumes measures that do not exist comparably (track
mobility, returns by pathway, employer credential requirements); as written it would be a measurement *agenda* with
six illustrations, not a dataset paper. *Resolution:* rank it #1 but reframe as "framework + measurement agenda +
country illustrations"; the theory paper is #2 conditional on rewrite.

**D3. THELEN vs all — HKG omission.**
THELEN: a typology that omits the case which actually fits the mechanism (HKG's rising stage gradient) is a warning
sign; Korea is placed in Regime II on institutional anecdotes, not measured status hierarchy. BRAY concurs. PATRINOS:
adding HKG is right but its incidence is second-hand/caveated; use the 2003 survey with the caveat.
*Resolution:* add HKG as an illustrative terminal-gate case with the 2003 caveat; revise Korea's cell or the mechanism.

**D4. CARTWRIGHT vs others — can the functional claim survive?**
CARTWRIGHT: a functional explanation can be retained only as an explicitly labelled hypothesis (consequences, not
intent), with the intentional/unintended distinction; the PH K-12 record shows a state raising a floor, not designing
overflow. Others concur; no dissent to recording the reversal (the paper currently slides from "states displace" to
"households do" without noticing).
