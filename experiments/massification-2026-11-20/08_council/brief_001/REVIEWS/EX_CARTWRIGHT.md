**G. GROUNDING.** Documented: *The Dappled World* (1999) argues that regularities are local and capacity-based, not
universal laws; *Evidence-Based Policy* (2012, with Hardie) argues that evidence must match the causal structure of
the policy being evaluated and that transfer requires theory of the local mechanism. **COUNCIL EXTRAPOLATION:** the
application to this brief is mine; neither book discusses credential pipelines. Basis: publication-level, not
fetched.

**M. MODEL RENDERING (KIDS).** My rendering: identify the capacity at issue — allocation of formal jobs by
positional rank — and the conditions under which it operates (a queue exists; credentials are required for the
queue; vacancies are fixed or slow to adjust; households cannot borrow freely). M1: households and employers;
M2: the capacity is a rule mapping rank to hire, not a law of markets; M3: the "parameters" (floor level, ranking
noise, vacancy path) are scope conditions, not estimates; M4: the observable implication is a *conditional*
statement: if a rank-ordered queue and fixed vacancies hold, then investment need not fall when returns fall; M5:
the paper's model is a formalization of this capacity, not a rival to it; M6: the capacity claim fails if, in cases
with rank queues, investment tracks measured returns; M7: KIDS demands the case description of the queue (which
employers rank, by what rule) before the capacity is invoked.

**B. THE CENTRAL DISTINCTION THE BRIEF NEEDS.** There are three different things in this batch and they must not be
merged: (i) *analytic consequences* of the coded rules (the landing of the floor, the displacement under fixed
slots); (ii) *model-class invariants* (what survives when assumptions vary — the brief does not establish any,
because no ensemble was run); (iii) *evidence about the world* (none is present; the country data are partial and
unused in the mechanism cells). The brief's question 1 asks which are (i). The honest answer: C2, C3, C4 and C5 are
(i) or mixed; only C1 is a check of the implementation. That is not shameful — a reproduction plus a set of
comparative statics is a legitimate contribution — but it must be labelled as such throughout, including in the
abstract.

**C. ESTIMAND.** The slope estimand is a comparative static over three demand levels chosen by the analyst; it is
not a causal parameter and not an elasticity. "Absolute income" and "formal rate" are model quantities. The
estimand that could carry a world claim is Prediction 2's *matched* graduate-supply shock — and that cell is absent.
