# REVIEW — BRAY composite (drawing on Bray & Lykins 2012 and Bray's comparative shadow-education work) — brief_001

**G. GROUNDING.** Documented: private supplementary tutoring is a large, growing, culturally embedded "shadow" of
mainstream schooling; it intensifies with competitive selection, is supplied by a mix of commercial firms and
school teachers, and raises equity concerns; comparative evidence across Hong Kong, Korea, mainland China, Cambodia
and Vietnam shows persistence and re-routing after policy interventions. *Shadow Education* (2012, with Lykins);
the HKU "Out of the Shadows" profile and the 2010 Beijing Forum interview record the same view. **COUNCIL
EXTRAPOLATION:** the model rendering below is mine; Bray does not formalize this in ABM. Basis: listed/publication;
pages not fetched in this environment.

**M. MODEL RENDERING (KIDS).** M1: households by income and location; mainstream schools with a gate; commercial
tutoring firms and teacher-tutors; a school-quality gradient. M2: tutoring demand rises with the stakes of the gate
and with the position of the pass line among takers, and with comparative social pressure; supply responds (entry,
price, teacher time), including teachers who tutor their own pupils. M3: measured shares where available; otherwise
described, not assumed. M4: gate-specific tutoring shares over time, supplier composition, equity incidence. M5: the
October model is a demand-side special case without suppliers, teacher-tutors, or school coupling. M6 falsifier:
tutoring shares that stay flat when a gate's stakes/bar change, or that collapse when supply is suppressed. M7 KIDS:
describe the actual tutoring market in the case before modeling it.

**A. VERDICTS.** C1 **ESTABLISHED** (reproduction matches). C2 **SIMULATION-ONLY**. C3 **SIMULATION-ONLY /
NOT ESTABLISHED** as a separation. C4 **SIMULATION-ONLY**. C5 **SIMULATION-ONLY, and largely imposed**: the
baseline shares (basic 0.05, apex 0.30, college 0.35, board 0.93; `01_sources/…/gates/findings.txt` line 3) are
properties of the coded tutoring cost/benefit functions and the payoff discontinuity. The bar sweep (0.03 at −2.0 to
0.77 at +0.5; line 5) is the fixed-cutoff mechanism in Yoo (2003) — C6 **ESTABLISHED** — so the "gate design is the
policy variable" conclusion is a restatement, not new evidence. C7 **ESTABLISHED**. C8/C9 **UNABLE TO ASSESS**.

**B. WHAT IS IMPOSED.** The result "tutoring clusters where the bar sits among takers" is arithmetically entailed by
a payoff discontinuity at the pass line plus any increasing tutoring cost. October does not model the
discontinuity — it codes it. That is legitimate as a simulation, but it cannot be called a finding about shadow
education. What would be a finding: that *endogenous* bar movement (employers raising requirements under screening
load) pulls tutoring with it — and that is not what C5 runs; C5 moves the bar exogenously.

**C. ESTIMAND.** "Share of takers who tutor" is a model construct with no observed counterpart in the brief; no
dispersion is reported for the gate table (stored, 4 replications per the manuscript; the brief gives no CI). The
"ban" rows (gates findings lines 6–8) show spending shifting, not disappearing — consistent with the comparative
record but generated by a coded informal-market substitute.

**D. TYPE CONFUSION.** C5 is labelled simulation; the October prose ("Prediction 3: gate clustering") reads as an
empirical prediction. Both can be true; the report must say which sentence is a model implication and which is a
testable prediction.

**E. STRONGEST DEFENSIBLE SENTENCE.** "In a model where gate payoffs are discontinuous at the pass line, tutoring
concentrates at the gate whose line sits among its takers, and raising a completion bar draws tutoring to it — a
simulation consistent with comparative shadow-education evidence but not evidence of it."

**F. STRONGEST NEGATIVE RESULT.** The model predicts basic-completion tutoring at 5 percent of takers. In the
comparative record, basic-education tutoring in Cambodia and the Philippines is not marginal: it is often the
largest market by volume. The model's ranking of gates may be right at the top (board exam) and wrong at the bottom,
because it omits teacher-tutors and the school-quality gradient. The brief does not notice this.

**G2. STEEL-MAN.** The rival is the comparative shadow-education record itself: tutoring is driven by social
competition, school quality and supplier incentives, and persists or re-routes after gate changes. A model that
reproduces gate clustering without suppliers can still be wrong about what to do — and the October paper's own
Double Reduction note (demand re-routed, not eliminated) is evidence the brief already knows this.

**H. ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATION.** Teacher-tutor supply (Jayachandran's incentive structure): when teachers can sell
supplementary lessons, tutoring appears even without gate-bar movement, and "school support/homework help" (the
China case) can reduce demand only if teacher and tutoring incentives change. This is testable with school-level
data and should be a mechanism arm.

**I. WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND; LIMITS.** Observed gate-specific tutoring shares before/after a real bar change;
supplier composition data; a model arm with teacher-tutors. I could not check the tutoring cost/benefit code, the
4-rep dispersion, or any external tutoring datum (data ledger partial).
