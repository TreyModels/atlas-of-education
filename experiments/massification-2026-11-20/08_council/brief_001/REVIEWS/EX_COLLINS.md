# REVIEW — COLLINS composite (drawing on Collins 1979/2019; Thurow 1975; Brown et al. 2011) — brief_001

**G. GROUNDING.** Documented: *The Credential Society* (1979; 2nd ed. 2019) argues that credentials expand through
status competition, that educational expansion is inflationary, and that schooling's main product is membership and
ranking rather than measured productivity. The 2018 Chinese-language interviews restate scepticism that education
equalizes. **COUNCIL EXTRAPOLATION:** the mapping to this brief is mine. Basis: publication plus listed interviews;
no full text fetched.

**M. MODEL RENDERING (KIDS).** My position as a model: a positional queue plus a credential stock-flow. Households
invest to defend or improve status; employers raise requirements when holders are abundant; the equilibrium is
inflation. M1: households by status group, credential stock, employer requirement level, annual hiring. M2:
P(hire) = rank in queue; requirement_t+1 = g(applicant/holder ratio); investment = defensive. M3: status weights
(assumed), employer screening cost (calibrated), no wage-productivity link needed. M4: observable = requirement level
rising while returns fall. M5: October's model is a special case with income-only preferences and no status groups.
M6 falsifier: requirement levels falling while the ratio of holders to jobs rises. M7 KIDS: describe the status
groups and their institutions; do not assume a generic taste for status.

**A. VERDICTS.** C1 **ESTABLISHED** (reproduction). C2 **SIMULATION-ONLY** — and the "discovery" is a restatement:
Collins (1979) already asserts that saturation intensifies competition; the model adds a decomposition but not a new
mechanism. C3 **NOT ESTABLISHED** as a theory-separating result (see E). C4 **SIMULATION-ONLY**, and the household
share (3.45 of 4.29 years) is consistent with the tradition's claim that households, not states, drive demand — but
that consistency is not evidence. C5 **NOT NOVEL** as shadow-education explanation: Yoo (2003) derives
effort-near-cutoff as rent seeking, Kim (2007) formalizes the Korean tutoring game; October's "gate line among its
takers" is that result restated. C6 **ESTABLISHED** (Yoo's result 3 is in the Yoo card, and the October prediction
is the ABM analogue). C7 **ESTABLISHED**. C8 **UNABLE TO ASSESS**. C9 **UNABLE TO ASSESS**.

**B. IMPOSED BY THE EQUATIONS.** Rank-order hiring is the mechanism, not a finding: the model's credibility
schedule and employer rule allocate by rank, so of course investment rises when rank matters more. More important:
the October model has no status groups and no credential stock-flow. It therefore cannot represent the thing Collins
actually claims — that *the demand for the credential is relational to the stock of other holders*, not to a wage.
What it models is a queue with income payoffs. That is Thurow, not Collins.

**C. ESTIMAND.** "College entry" is not credential attainment; entry slope across three demand levels is not an
attainment response. Q3–5 "college entry" is also a household decision, not a queue outcome — the estimand mixes
household demand with employer allocation.

**D. TYPE CONFUSION.** The brief labels C2 simulation; the paper's prose ("the models separate the mechanisms")
is acceptable for models, but "explains intensification" is a claim about the world. Do not let the footnote carry
the whole caveat.

**E. THE STRONGEST DEFENSIBLE CLAIM.** Not "ranking causes intensification" (that is my thesis restated) but: *the
floor and the race are distinct objects with distinct contributions — the floor explains exclusion where no ranked
contest exists; only its movement adds intensification above a ranked queue.* That separation is a real conceptual
advance over Thurow, and it is the paper's best candidate. The novelty is in the decomposition, not the sign.

**F. STRONGEST NEGATIVE RESULT.** The floor's own contribution to completion is ~4 points (0.733 → 0.771) while its
exclusion effect is enormous (formal|dip 0.433 → 0.116, floor_test.log lines 12/11). And the actor knockouts show the
household leg dominant (3.45/4.29), the employer leg negligible under weak skill returns (0.04, necessity findings
line 28). If the paper's title concept is the relational floor, the result is that *the floor is not where
intensification lives* — which is an honest and useful finding.

**G2. STEEL-MAN.** The rival is not world culture here; it is the labour-queue tradition itself (Thurow), plus
Collins: "you have simulated credential inflation and called it a floor." The ABM's counter is the floor/race
decomposition and the many-readers Philippine case — not the sign.

**H. ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATION.** Status consumption: families buy credentials for relative position even with zero
income return. The model, with income-only preferences, cannot rule this out; a status-weighted utility arm would.

**I. WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND; LIMITS.** Evidence that household investment responds to the *stock of other
holders* independent of wages (e.g., enrolment responses to cohort size at fixed returns). I could not check the
model's household utility code or the Philippine case's reader-specific floors.
