## 4. What the pipeline reclassification means for the next model line

**4.1 Does the v1.2 demographic/quality layer still earn its place?** **Yes — at the floor and lower stage; no — as the
ceiling's cause.** Three verified reasons.
1. The layer's own object is rationing, and rationing exists **only** where it was designed to act: primary **87.4%** and
   lower-secondary **93.3%** of level-years are officially rationed, upper **6.5%**, tertiary **0%** (V3).
2. The model's one plausible level match is **primary** (median deviation **−4.30 pp**, V1), the most heavily rationed
   stage; its misses are **lower −29.44** and **upper −27.59**.
3. CEIL-ATTRIB's Test A is `MIXED`: the mechanism moves the upper 2024 level (work incentive **+6.27 pp**, γ=0
   **+2.64 pp**, flat cohort **−3.47 pp**) but so does the calibration (`m=1.05` **−3.48**, build-out **+2.08/−7.56**).
   "**The mechanism produces the ceiling**" is therefore **not licensed** — and the ceiling is **not** a calibration
   artefact either. The honest state is: surface jointly sensitive, not uniquely attributed.
   **Ruling:** KEEP A2 as a **floor/lower-stage** block; **KILL** its use as the upper ceiling's mechanism account.

**4.2 Is the lower-secondary pipeline now the object to model?** **Yes, as a ratio — and the model cannot yet hold
one.** Three constraints the next line must carry.
1. **The object is `R = upper_t / lower_{t−3}`** (a transition ratio), not "the lower level". PHL spans **0.889–1.072**,
   mostly **0.93–0.97** (near-proportional); KHM **0.139–0.691** (gated); CHN **0.645–0.966** (rising); KOR
   **0.831–0.996**. The ceiling question becomes: *what sets a system's transition ratio?* — not *what blocks the upper
   gate?* No direction claim is made.
2. **One numerator only.** Completion is lagged-attainment; GER is not substitutable (V6: PHL completion/GER 0.90–1.09;
   GER-based upper/lower ratios in band for **1/15** PHL, **0/15** CHN, **0/8** KHM, against **23/23** KOR). The object is
