# REVIEW — PATRINOS composite (drawing on Psacharopoulos & Patrinos 2018; Becker 1964; steel-man seat) — brief_003

**G. GROUNDING.** Literature basis: private returns are substantial, heterogeneous, and decline with expansion;
competitive wage-setting is the right null, not a straw man. **COUNCIL EXTRAPOLATION:** the F1/F2 assessment is the
composite's.

**M. MODEL RENDERING (rival at its best).** M1 both worlds share the formal/informal margin, vacancy path and shock;
M2 the rival hires by expected productivity under CES wages with σ; M3 for a Δ comparison the worlds must match on
*both* the formal share and the income scale (else the "same outcome" is defined in different economies); M4 outcome:
Δ formal-employment probability and Δ income; M5 surplus = slot-rationed σ→∞ case; M6 falsifier: a level-matched rival
showing a formal-probability fall comparable to the surplus world; M7 KIDS: describe the allocation rule in both worlds.

**A. VERDICTS.** F1 **CONDITIONALLY ESTABLISHED (simulation-only)** — but only with the level-match caveat in the
same sentence. The clean cell is σ=2, where the rival's degree income is matched (2.706 vs 2.925) and its formal
response is −0.0054 (±0.0027) against the surplus −0.1511 (±0.0217); at σ=0.5 and σ=1 the formal share is matched
(0.279–0.280 vs 0.286) but the income scale is **not** (11.388 and 1.457 vs 2.925), so those cells mix a matched
formal share with an unmatched wage level. Sign-off language must therefore say "no material fall (≤0.5 pp at
σ=2, the fully matched cell)" — **not** "does not fall at σ ≤ 2". F2 **AMEND**: "E13b's income contradiction was a
level artefact" is too exculpatory. B1 (N=3000, not blind) reproduces a σ=0.5 income fall (−0.109, −0.175) beyond
2 seed sd with a plausible baseline ratio (0.234), while its σ=0.5 corner is numerically unstable (baseline ratio
0.022–0.055, degree income 1.0–42.4 across seeds). The honest status is **unidentified at high substitutability**,
not "artefact". Do not cite the σ=0.5 corner to win either way.

**B. ASSUMPTIONS / FAIRNESS.** The formal-access margin is partly definitional: the surplus world rations by
credential, the rival hires by productivity. So the 15-point gap is "rationed vs productivity hiring", a model-class
implication, not evidence that real markets ration. It is nevertheless a fair *conditional* statement, because the
level match now holds at one σ.

**C. ESTIMAND.** Δ formal probability is comparable only after matching both levels; the report should show the
matching column. Income changes are in different units at σ<2; do not compare them.

**D. TYPE.** All simulation; the summary correctly labels it. The sign-off sentence must carry "simulation-only".

**E. SIGN-OFF SENTENCE.** "In this model class, with baseline formal access matched, a graduate-supply shock under
fixed vacancies lowers diploma holders' formal-employment probability by 15.1 points under credential-rationed
hiring, while a productivity-hiring CES benchmark shows no material fall (largest −0.5 pp, at σ=2 where degree income
is also matched); absolute income does not discriminate, and the σ=0.5 income corner is numerically unstable."

**F. NEGATIVE.** At the one fully matched cell the rival's diploma income **rises** +12.5 points: the income
discriminator is not merely noisy, it points the other way where the levels are clean.

**G2. STEEL-MAN (my seat).** The rival wins on income; it does not win on formal access at σ=2. That is the only
surviving discriminating margin, and it is one cell deep.

**H. ALTERNATIVE.** Search-and-matching with endogenous vacancy posting could reverse the formal-access gap; not run.

**I. WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND.** A blind, fully level-matched rival at every σ (both formal share and income),
4–6 paired reps, predeclared half-gap rule — the first credibility experiment below.
