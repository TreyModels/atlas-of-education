evidence about any country."

## Three credibility-raising experiments

1. **Blind, fully level-matched rival.** Arms: surplus vs CES rival with σ ∈ {0.5, 1, 2}, matching *both* baseline
   formal share and degree-income scale at every σ, same vacancy path; 6 paired reps; predeclared blind (no prior look
   at stored output). Estimand: Δ formal|dip; threshold: rival fall < half the surplus fall at ≥2 σ including the
   matched cell; cost ~6 min + one predeclaration file. This is the only experiment that can make F1 more than one
   cell deep.
2. **Substitution-elasticity identification (E18c).** Arms: ts grid {0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2} × {universal, targeted 40%,
   capacity 25%}; estimand: ts* boundary at which the sign of Δ paid spend flips, and whether the plausible empirical
   range spans it; threshold: report ts* with seed intervals and the empirical anchor needed to bound it (China/Korea
   homework-and-tutoring series); cost ~3 min simulation + data work. Converts the F3 ambiguity into a measured
   parameter to chase.
3. **Philippine calibration with a held-out period.** Fit ≤8 parameters to PH 1980–2010 attainment/enrolment by
   level; hold out 2011–2024; estimand: held-out MAE and shape correlation by level; threshold: predeclared holdout
   MAE ≤ in-sample + 2 pp and corr > 0; cost ~20–30 min + data. This is the only route from F7's description to a
   validation claim, and it is what a referee will ask for.
