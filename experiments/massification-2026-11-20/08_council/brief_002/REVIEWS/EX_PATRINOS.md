# REVIEW — PATRINOS composite (drawing on Psacharopoulos & Patrinos 2018; Becker 1964; steel-man seat) — brief_002

**G. GROUNDING.** Literature basis: private returns to schooling are substantial, heterogeneous and decline with
expansion; the competitive null is a real benchmark, not a straw man. **COUNCIL EXTRAPOLATION:** the E13b assessment
is the composite's.

**M. MODEL RENDERING (the rival at its best).** M1 households by quintile, CES wage setting with σ, a formal and an
informal sector, credit constraints and option value. M2 investment responds to expected marginal return. M3 σ, the
informal wage and the vacancy path are explicit, and *baseline levels* (formal share, income scale) must be matched
across worlds for Δ comparisons to mean anything. M4 outcome: Δ diploma holders' income and Δ formal probability.
M5 nesting: the surplus world is the σ→∞, slot-rationed case. M6 falsifier: matched supply shock that raises diploma
income in the rival while the surplus world lowers it — and vice versa. M7 KIDS: the same institutions in both worlds.

**A. VERDICTS.** C16/C17 **CONTRADICTED as originally stated**: the matched rival produces zero/negative diploma
income changes at σ ≤ 1 (E13b findings: σ=0.5 −0.059, σ=1.0 −0.034 and −0.031; surplus −0.061 and −0.042). The
income discriminator fails. But three precision/comparability caveats must be stated: (i) the σ=0.5 fall is
−0.059 ± 0.059 (1 sd) and the σ=1.0 fall is ≈1.6 sd from zero — a 2-sd test would not reject zero; (ii) the worlds
are not level-matched: baseline formal rates 0.286 (surplus) vs 0.463 (rival), and rival degree income is 32.3
(sd 23.3) vs 2.93 — a Δ comparison across these scales is not a like-for-like test; (iii) the brief's quoted C17
numbers do not match the artifact (brief: rival −0.119/−0.124 at σ=0.5; artifact: −0.059/−0.015; surplus brief
−0.037/−0.019 vs artifact −0.061/−0.042) and the brief still lists E13b at reps=1 although it ran reps=6. Quote the
artifact. C18 **pilot-strength** (reps=3, N=2000). C19 **disqualified as a country claim**.

**B. WHAT IS IMPOSED.** The surplus world's formal-probability fall (−0.154) is a slot-rationing identity: more
graduates, fixed slots, credential rank. The rival's formal probability is ≈0 by construction because it hires by
productivity, not credentials. So the *formal-probability discriminator* is "which allocation rule is coded", not a
free-standing result — defensible only as a conditional model-class statement, and only if baseline formal shares are
matched.

**C. ESTIMAND.** Income changes are in different units across worlds (2.93 vs 32.3 degree income); formal rates start
from different levels. Δ formal probability is comparable only after baseline matching.

**D. TYPE CONFUSION.** The brief says "the income discriminator fails" — accurate — but the framing "only the income
framing is killed" overstates what survives: the formal-probability gap is currently a coded-rule difference.

**E. STRONGEST DEFENSIBLE SENTENCE.** "In two matched model architectures, a graduate-supply shock lowers diploma
holders' absolute income in both; the income discriminator therefore fails. The share of diploma holders in formal
employment falls sharply only where hiring is credential-rationed, and that comparison currently rests on different
baseline formal shares."

**F. STRONGEST NEGATIVE RESULT.** The one symmetric result: the *ratio* of diploma to degree income rises in both
worlds (brief 001 point). If even the ratio is architecture-insensitive, Prediction 2's only candidate discriminators
are formal probability and its incidence, not wages.

**G2. STEEL-MAN (my seat).** The competitive world with a formal margin **wins on income**. It does not yet win on
formal probability, but the comparison is not level-matched and the precision at σ=0.5 is poor. Batch B should run a
baseline-matched arm (equal formal share and equal income scale) before any claim survives.

**H. ALTERNATIVE.** Search-and-matching with endogenous vacancy posting: a supply shock can create formal vacancies,
reversing displacement without any credential mechanism.

**I. WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND.** E13c: baseline-matched rival, same formal share and income scale, 4–6 paired reps,
σ grid; predeclared: formal-probability fall in the rival < half the surplus fall → discriminator survives.
