# REVIEW — BRAY composite (drawing on Bray & Lykins 2012; Bray's comparative work; Bray & Zhang) — brief_002

**G. GROUNDING.** Literature basis: comparative shadow-education research — tutoring is gate-anchored but also
culturally embedded, supplier-driven (including teacher-tutors), and it re-routes after suppression; equity effects
are first-order. **COUNCIL EXTRAPOLATION:** the specific assessment of E18/E7 is the composite's.

**M. MODEL RENDERING.** A shadow-education model that could test a Double Reduction hypothesis needs: M1 households,
mainstream schools, gate line, commercial tutors, teacher-tutors, informal/illegal market; M2 tutoring demand rises
with the distance to the gate line, the stakes, comparative pressure and household resources; supply responds to
price and to teacher incentives; M3 measured shares where available; M4 observed gate-specific take-up, spend and
supplier composition; M5 the October/massabm model is a demand-side special case without suppliers or school
coupling; M6 falsifier: a support/homework regime that lowers tutoring take-up at fixed stakes; M7 KIDS: describe the
actual Chinese 2021 bundle before coding it.

**A. VERDICTS.** C10 **SIMULATION-ONLY and not a test of the policy**: E18's support is a free +0.5 sd score module,
universal, with no demand channel, no homework reduction, no teacher-time change, no capacity limit. The finding that
it raises paid spend (4092 → 4815) is a positional-race result, not evidence against Trey's hypothesis. C11
**SIMULATION-ONLY**: the ban/leak accounting is coded (leak=0.5); "88% of spend persists under an all-formal ban" is
a scenario conditional on that leak, not a comparative finding. C14 **NOT NOVEL**: the bar sweep is Yoo (2003); the
residual is ban/leak incidence, which is assumption-driven. C19 **PILOT, no country claim** — and this matters most
for a report about Double Reduction, because no Chinese series is loaded.

**B. WHAT IS IMPOSED.** E18 has a structural feature the brief understates: with universal support, the apex gate's
positional premium *rises* because more students crowd toward the cut-offs — a property of rank gates, not of the
tutoring market. Bray's comparative record would predict the same direction only under conditions the model does not
code: where tutoring is content-substitutable and where households are budget-limited. In Cambodia/Philippines,
basic-level tutoring is a mass market (the model gives 0.040 at the basic gate); that is a KIDS failure to describe
the market before modeling it, and it weakens the gate ranking as a description.

**C. ESTIMAND.** "Paid spend" excludes unpaid family help, school homework time and teacher tutoring — precisely the
channels Trey's hypothesis is about. A model that measures only paid spend cannot adjudicate a substitution
hypothesis whose mechanism is unpaid.

**D. TYPE CONFUSION.** C10 is labelled simulation; the risk is that the summary sentence "school support raises paid
tutoring spend" travels to China. It must not.

**E. STRONGEST DEFENSIBLE SENTENCE.** "In a model with universal free exam support but no demand-side relief, paid
tutoring spend rises at the apex gate; this is a positional-race implication, not evidence that Double Reduction
increased tutoring in China."

**F. STRONGEST NEGATIVE RESULT.** The partial-access arm (A5, 60% coverage) reduces paid spend vs baseline (−236.5):
targeting, not universalism, is what allows substitution in this model. The brief does not notice that the one arm
resembling the actual policy (targeted, partly enforced) is the only one pointing toward Trey's hypothesis.

**G2. STEEL-MAN.** Bray's comparative evidence: bans re-route demand (Korea 1980, China 2021) and suppliers adapt;
the model's leak parameter is a crude version of this. A supplier/teacher-tutor arm is the fair test.

**H. ALTERNATIVE.** Teacher-tutor model (Jayachandran): teachers paid to tutor their own pupils; school support that
changes teacher time can reduce tutoring without a ban. Runnable as an E18b arm.

**I. WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND.** E18b with targeted support and a teacher-tutor channel; observed Chinese
tutoring-spend and take-up series 2018–2024 with the ban and school-support components separated.
