# REVIEW — BRAY composite (drawing on Bray & Lykins 2012; Bray & Kwok 2003; Bray's comparative work) — brief_007

**G. GROUNDING.** Literature basis: shadow education's form follows the gate and the supplier — commercial mass
lectures where a terminal exam dominates and family income is high; teacher-sold tutoring where public teachers can
sell the curriculum; participation gradients by stage reflect which gate is binding and who supplies. **COUNCIL
EXTRAPOLATION:** the ratings below are the composite's.

**A. WHAT EACH PROFILE SUPPORTS.**
- **Korea: calibration anchor, not incidence evidence.** Official 2023 by-level rates (86.0/75.4/66.4), hours,
  spend per student/participant and provider spend mix are high-confidence; they can calibrate provider *form and
  fees*. But U1 already shows the model's stage ordering is inverted (model rising E<M<H; official falling), so KOR
  is not yet usable as an incidence check.
- **Hong Kong: check/OOS.** The 2003 peer-reviewed gradient (35.1/46.6/70.3) is the only defensible stage anchor;
  U1b shows shape-only success (monotone) but level and provider-order failures. 1996/2010 stay low-confidence;
  2010's 85% is the single calibration moment.
- **Japan: within-country check.** Grade-6 mean 41.8% with a 22.1–57.9% prefecture range is high-confidence and
  offers the cleanest variation; fees unresolved.
- **China: bounded prior only.** 2014 national 29.8% (medium), post-2021 ">80% did not attend" is an inequality, not
  a rate; no provider split. Use as a bound, never a target.
- **Cambodia: case demonstration.** One district, six schools, teacher-sold (Rien Kuo). Good for a mechanism, not a
  national rate.
- **Philippines: data-first only.** Incidence blocked; one 2010 select sample. Cohort/economy series exist.

**B. SLATE.** I would run, in order: (1) **provider-form and fee technology** (KOR + HKG): mass-lecture vs 1:1 vs
teacher, scale economies, fixed cost/capacity, fee/income ratio — because both loop-4 OOS failures are provider-order
failures; (2) **gate-stage gradient** (KOR falling vs HKG rising) as a structural test of where the binding gate sits;
(3) **teacher-tutor channel** (KHM): teachers sell the curriculum — reproduce the urban–rural gap and teacher share;
(4) **income gradient** (KOR bands 183 vs 671k won; HK 1.1–5% of income). The provider experiment is the one that
would most change the paper, because "shadow education" in this programme is currently provider-blind.

**C. RELATION SLOTS.** All PENDING (09_relations); design intent: provider technology = plausibly-causal;
teacher-tutor = plausibly-causal (case scope); gate gradient = plausibly-causal; income gradient = correlational.

**D. TYPE.** Only the within-country and cross-stage designs can be identification; cross-country level comparisons
are description.
