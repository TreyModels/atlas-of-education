# COUNCIL DELIBERATION 011 — merits and testability of the overflow-governance theory

**Panel (AI composites):** COLLINS (credential theory), BRAY (shadow education, mandatory), THELEN (comparative
institutions), PAGE (test design; steel-man seat).
**Input:** paper v1/v2; brief 010; on-disk data (`04_data/shadow_round2/`, `SHADOW_TARGETS.csv`,
`PARAMETER_MAP.csv`, `09_relations/`, `LOOP_CLOSEOUT.md`, briefs 003–009).

## Agreed: the merits, ranked

1. **The reframing is the contribution.** Treating shadow education as governance/legitimation infrastructure rather
   than market supplement or policy failure is genuinely useful and connects the phenomenon to massification's
   inclusion/distinction tension. It is the reason this is more than another credential-inflation paper.
2. **The legitimation/consent claim is the distinctive theoretical idea** (COLLINS's emphasis, accepted by all): that
   participation converts structural exclusion into moralised individual effort — and thereby stabilises the system —
   is a claim about beliefs and consent, not just behaviour. It is untested; it is also testable.
3. **The two/three-margin insight is the best empirical fragment in the paper**, though undeveloped: Korea's
   participation falls with stage while spend per student rises; HKG's participation rises with stage. The paper
   stumbles on a real structural distinction (participation × hours × price) that deserves to be formalised.
4. **The typology is a measurement agenda, not a validated classification** (THELEN's emphasis): differentiation and
   responsiveness could be operationalised (vocational share, certified pathways, track mobility, returns by pathway,
   employer credential requirement; capacity-binding flag), and then tested; currently the cells are labels.
5. **The policy predictions are the immediately testable part:** bans re-route; transition requires status
   recalibration; Regime I containment; ritual persistence.

## Agreed: what must be credited, and what is not new

The queue/inflation mechanism is Collins (1979) + Hirsch (1976), and needs Thurow (1975) and Spence/Arrow as rivals.
The comparative shadow-education phenomenon is Bray and colleagues; the post-socialist/area cases are Silova, Sobhy and
others. The governance/legitimation framing and the typology are the paper's own additions. "Extended phenotype" is
rhetoric, not mechanism. "States displace" is a functional hypothesis, not an observable act.

## Disagreement: which test first

**COLLINS:** the legitimation claim is the only truly novel claim; test it first (vignette experiment), or the paper
publishes a familiar credential-inflation argument with a new vocabulary.
**BRAY:** the Korea three-margin decomposition is cheapest, uses official data already on disk, and can falsify the
paper's stated Regime II mechanism (if price/hour explains the spend rise while participation falls, the "arms race
participation" story is wrong).
**PAGE:** both are complementary — the decomposition tests the mechanism description; the vignette tests the novel
theory. If only one, the decomposition, because it is near-zero cost and decisive for what the paper says about Korea.
**THELEN:** if the paper is to be comparative, the typology measurement (two indices across the six-country cards and
the scout panel) is the scientific content; otherwise the typology remains decoration.

**Resolution:** report two tracks — (A) cheap mechanism test first: Korea decomposition (and its policy-event
extension if the KEDI annual series can be acquired); (B) distinctive theory test: legitimation vignette. The
typology measurement is the paper's framework contribution, ranked third by feasibility, not by importance. COLLINS's
dissent is recorded: he would invert A and B.

## Disagreement: cut or keep the ritual chapter

**COLLINS/BRAY:** keep the moral-economy argument but rebuild it on motive/norm data; drop the Durkheimian scaffolding
that implies ritual *is* the mechanism.
**THELEN:** keep it as a hypothesis about legitimation, not as a description of practice.
**PAGE:** cut anything that cannot fail; the ritual chapter as written cannot.

**Resolution:** keep one section (legitimation hypothesis + test design), cut the four-feature ritual anatomy into a
single paragraph, drop "extended phenotype".
