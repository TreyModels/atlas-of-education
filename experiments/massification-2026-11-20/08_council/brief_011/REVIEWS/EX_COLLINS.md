# REVIEW — COLLINS composite (drawing on Collins 1979/2019; Interaction Ritual Chains 2004; Hirsch 1976) — brief_011

**G. GROUNDING.** Literature basis: credential inflation and status competition are Collins (1979); positional goods
are Hirsch (1976); interaction ritual is Collins (2004). **COUNCIL EXTRAPOLATION:** the novelty assessment is the
composite's.

**A. MERITS, RANKED.** (1) The **reframing** is real and useful: shadow education as governance/legitimation
infrastructure rather than market supplement or policy failure. (2) The **legitimation claim** — that participation
converts structural exclusion into moralised individual effort and thereby stabilises the system — is the paper's one
genuinely distinct theoretical contribution; it is a claim about beliefs and consent, not just behaviour, and it is
testable. (3) The **ritual/moral-economy** mechanism is connective and plausible, but asserted. (4) The typology is a
heuristic agenda, not a validated classification. (5) The policy predictions are the most immediately testable.

**B. NOVELTY.** The queue/inflation mechanism is not new: it is Collins (1979) plus Hirsch (1976), and it needs
Thurow (1975) and Spence/Arrow (signalling) as the rival it must beat. The paper's addition is the *governance*
framing and the *legitimation* function — that is where its originality lives, and the paper currently spends too
much space re-deriving the queue.

**C. WHAT IS WORTH TESTING.** The distinctive test is a **legitimation study**: do families who invest more in tutoring
(especially those who fail to reach elite outcomes) report higher belief in meritocratic fairness? Design options:
(i) a vignette survey experiment (randomise tutoring investment in a described family; measure perceived fairness of
selection) — cheap, feasible, falsifiable; (ii) add motive + legitimacy items to an existing national survey; (iii)
cross-national ecological check using WVS/Asian Barometer legitimacy items against tutoring prevalence (weak, but
cheap). Threshold: a predeclared effect size on the fairness item; null result falsifies the paper's distinctive claim.

**D. WHAT TO CUT.** "Extended phenotype" (metaphor, not mechanism); "states displace" as an intentional claim (it is a
functional hypothesis); the Regime I claims until sourced. Keep the legitimation/moral-economy argument — it is the
reason to publish this paper rather than another credential-inflation paper.
