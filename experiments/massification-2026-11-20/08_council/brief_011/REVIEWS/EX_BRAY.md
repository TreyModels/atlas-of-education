# REVIEW — BRAY composite (drawing on Bray & Lykins 2012; Bray & Kwok 2003; Bray's comparative work) — brief_011

**G. GROUNDING.** Literature basis: shadow education's stage/provider structure is documented in the comparative
record; Korea's official survey and HKG's 2003 survey are the best cases; Cambodia is teacher-sold and case-study
scoped. **COUNCIL EXTRAPOLATION:** the test ranking is the composite's.

**A. MERITS.** The paper's best move is treating shadow education as structural rather than anomalous — that is
continuous with, and a useful extension of, the comparative literature (Bray's own work) because it connects
household demand to system governance. Its provider and stage material is where the empirical value is. The
"moral economy" language matches what families say in the field ("necessary tension", education fever), but the paper
asserts it rather than measuring it.

**B. WHAT IS TESTABLE WITH DATA ON DISK.** (1) **Korea three-margin decomposition:** participation 86.0 → 75.4 →
66.4, hours 7.5 → 7.4 → 6.7, spend per student 398 → 449 → 491k won (`shadow_round2` §1). Spend = participation ×
hours × price/hour; implied price/hour rises ~37% elementary→high. That tests the paper's "arms race" story directly:
the rise is a *price* phenomenon, not extra hours. (2) **HKG 2003 stage gradient** (35.1/46.6/70.3) as the
terminal-gate case against Korea's falling profile — a two-case descriptive test of the gate-architecture claim.
(3) **Provider form:** KOR hagwon 241/434k won spend; HKG mass-lecture 72.4% at S6-7; KHM teacher-sold (case) — the
paper's Regime II vs IV provider prediction is testable descriptively. (4) **Ban re-routing:** China post-2021 is
headline-only (<20% did not attend; low confidence), Korea curfew/fee-cap episodes need the KEDI PEES annual series;
this is a data-first test, not a desk test.

**C. WHAT IS NOT WORTH TESTING (yet).** Regime I "contained optimisation" — no verified incidence data here; it is a
case-narrative, not a test. "Ritual persistence" without motive items is unfalsifiable.

**D. ONE TEST FIRST.** The Korea decomposition: free data, on disk, and it can falsify the paper's stated Regime II
mechanism (if price/hour explains the spend rise while participation falls, the "arms race participation" story is
wrong or needs the two-margin rewrite). Keep the Cambodia case as the paper's empirical anchor; do not claim it
national.
