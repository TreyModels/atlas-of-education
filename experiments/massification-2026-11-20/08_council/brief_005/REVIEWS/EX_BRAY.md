# REVIEW — BRAY composite (drawing on Bray & Lykins 2012; Bray's comparative work; Bray & Zhang) — brief_005

**G. GROUNDING.** Literature basis: shadow education is stage-specific and gate-anchored; provider mix is
compositional (commercial firms, teacher-sold classes, informal); participation rates are survey estimates with
different denominators; East Asian systems have high upper-secondary rates, Cambodia's shadow is teacher-sold at the
Grade-9 gate, and the Philippines' spend is concentrated at college-entrance review centres. **COUNCIL
EXTRAPOLATION:** the comparisons below are the composite's.

**M. MODEL RENDERING.** For "shadow appears where the literature says": M1 stage-specific take-up (primary, lower
secondary, senior secondary, college gate), M2 provider composition as an outcome, M3 spending/hours gradients,
M4 predeclared national ranges with definitions, M5 a disclosed miss per cell. The loop-2 module has M1 partly
(stage take-up S and C), M2 yes, M3 partly, but reports only senior-secondary and college-gate take-up at y40; it does
not report primary/lower-secondary take-up, which is where two of the strongest cards live.

**A. VERDICTS.** **Shadow incidence: NOT ESTABLISHED.** Of the 11 `use_as_model_target=YES` rows, exactly one (HK
senior-secondary 0.85) is used — as the calibration target. The other ten (KOR 78.5/90; JPN 41.8; HKG 85 is the
fitted one; KHM 68.4/41.3/60.5/26.4; PHL 46.5/23.8/28.1) are not reported as checks. Stage-matched comparison is
even worse than absence: the model's PH college-gate take-up is 0.899 against a literature upper-secondary
review-centre rate of 28.1%, and its KHM senior-secondary rate is 0.887 against a Grade-9 rate of 68.4% and primary
41.3% (urban 60.5 / rural 26.4). So the take-up pattern the literature reports is **contradicted**, not merely
unverified: the one fitted parameter makes the shadow sector near-universal in all three countries.

**B. WHAT IS IMPOSED.** The fit is a one-parameter mapping from HK 0.85 to a common household-demand intercept; the
PH/KHM levels follow mechanically from that one fit (§4), and the uncalibrated run puts HK at 0.581 (a −26.9 pp miss).
Provider composition (teacher-tutors 76/86/97%) is generated by declared capacity/fixed-cost values and is not
calibrated to any provider data — but it is qualitatively the most interesting positive: it reproduces the
comparative fact that the shadow is teacher-sold in Cambodia and largely teacher-supplied in the Philippines,
while in HK commercial firms coexist. Do not claim it as a finding without provider data.

**C. ESTIMAND.** Take-up share by stage and provider; units must be "share of that stage's students". The literature
rates are not all senior-secondary shares; comparing a college-gate take-up with a senior-secondary review-centre
rate is a definitional mismatch unless stated.

**D. TYPE.** The HK 0.85 match is a calibration, not validation. The cards are measurement; the model is a
conditional generator.

**E. STRONGEST DEFENSIBLE SENTENCE.** "The module generates a teacher-tutor-dominated shadow sector whose
senior-secondary take-up can be set to the one verified HK number by a single demand intercept; it does not
reproduce the documented stage/provider levels in the Philippines or Cambodia, and the cross-country pattern is
not identified by one moment."

**F. NEGATIVE.** The model's PH/KHM take-up levels (88–89%) are four times the Philippine survey rate and well above
the Cambodian primary rates — the strongest contradiction in the batch, and it should be reported as a miss, not
omitted because "no verified target" exists.

**G2. STEEL-MAN.** The genuine achievement: the provider side is modelled as entry with capacity, not fixed shares,
and teacher-tutors emerge as the dominant supplier — exactly what Bray's comparative record leads one to expect.

**H. ALTERNATIVE.** Tutoring may respond to school quality and social competition more than to gate position; the
positional-response test is null here, which is consistent with that reading.

**I. WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND.** Stage-matched take-up checks for PH/KHM/KOR/JPN with predeclared ranges; provider
composition data; an honest miss table.
