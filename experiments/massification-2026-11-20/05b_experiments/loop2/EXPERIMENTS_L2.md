# EXPERIMENTS — Loop 2: bidirectional education–economy + endogenous shadow sector

**NEW module, clearly named:** `loop2/code/twoway.py` — a declared compact cohort-flow model with
education→economy, economy→education, and a shadow sector with provider entry (commercial firms and
teacher-tutors), price tatonnement, capacity and class-size dilution. It is NOT massabm, NOT floorworld,
NOT triad, and NOT a reproduction of any paper result. Predeclaration: `PRE_DECLARATIONS_L2.md` (with
amendments L2-1…L2-5, incl. the Council brief-004 binding tests added after the first run). Outputs:
`runs/L2/` (calibrated shadow-demand run, 150 trajectories), `runs/L2_uncalibrated/` (sensitivity run).
All numbers are **stylized model output**; no imputed data cell is used as a target.

**Status.** completed: 3 countries × 10 regimes × 5 matched seeds × 40 years = 150 trajectories,
**0 failures** (first pass had 15 failures from a fixed code defect; discarded, see `ERROR_LOG_L2.md`).
Headline table `SUMMARY_L2.csv`; manifest `RUN_MANIFEST_L2.json`.

## 2. Council binding test 1 — freeze-both symmetry (the central result)

**(i) Freeze schooling — does the generated formal/complex job path still rise?** YES, strongly:
complex **vacancies** under frozen schooling rise PH 71.0→130.9 (+60.0), HK 172.8→235.8 (+63.0),
KHM 32.3→80.0 (+47.7) over the 40 years. With endogenous schooling the y40 vacancies are 135.4 / 242.3 /
83.1 — i.e. schooling adds only **+4.5 / +6.5 / +3.1 jobs (≈3–4% of the job level)**. The formal/complex
job path is therefore **overwhelmingly supplied by the declared demand trend, not generated by education**.

**(ii) Freeze economy (R10) — does attainment still rise?** YES, but much less: completion y10→y40
+0.028 (PH), +0.022 (HK), +0.024 (KHM) with the economy frozen (vacancies and beliefs fixed at t=0),
versus +0.067 / +0.118 / +0.077 with the live economy. The frozen-economy rise is the shadow-sector
channel (tutoring supply/prices move schooling even without wages/jobs). So **economy→education is the
dominant direction on the education side, but there is a residual non-economy channel ≈ 20–40% of the
attainment rise**.

**Verdict.** Not a strong two-way model: the job side is approximately a schedule (3–4% generated), the
education side is a genuine model driven mainly by demand. This is exactly the Council's "if neither
moves, the two-way claim is a schedule" concern — here only the education side really moves.

## 3. Council binding test 2 — provider supply / entry, teacher-tutor capacity (structural variable)

Explicitly modelled: provider entry cost `F_cm/F_tt`, capacity `cap_cm/cap_tt`, entry speeds, price
tatonnement with a capacity–quality dilution term; commercial firms vs **teacher-tutors** are separate
provider types with lower capacity and lower fixed cost (teacher-tutors enter faster). **Provider mix is
dominated by teacher-tutors: 76% of providers (PH), 86% (HK), 97% (KHM)** at y40. Endogenous entry raises
senior-secondary take-up by 46–66pp relative to the fixed-supply counterfactual (PH 0.884 vs 0.219;
HK 0.865 vs 0.327; KHM 0.887 vs 0.258). Caveat: in endogenous entry the price collapses toward its
declared floor (0.30/0.55/0.13 vs initial 1.0/2.5/0.5) and provider counts grow 5–90× — the entry dynamics
overshoot profitability, so the price/entry block needs calibration against price moments before its
levels are read as economics.

## 4. Council binding test 3 — take-up and provider mix are CHECKS, not fitted shares

One declared calibration moment: the common household-demand intercept is fitted so **HK senior-secondary
take-up = 0.85** (Kwo & Bray 2011, with the authors' "not statistically robust" caveat). Provider entry
costs, capacities and teacher-tutor capacity are held at declared values.

| country (y40) | senior-sec take-up | college-gate take-up | tutoring price | commercial / teacher providers | teacher share | fixed-supply take-up (counterfactual) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PH-like | 0.884 (0.005) | 0.899 (0.006) | 0.30 | 23.0 / 72.4 | 0.76 | 0.219 |
| HK-like | **0.865** (0.004) [calibrated to 0.85 target] | 0.954 (0.001) | 0.55 | 14.3 / 87.2 | 0.86 | 0.327 |
| KHM-like | 0.887 (0.007) | 0.793 (0.016) | 0.13 | 2.0 / 76.5 | 0.97 | 0.258 |

Uncalibrated sensitivity (`runs/L2_uncalibrated/`): senior-sec take-up PH 0.643 / HK **0.581** / KHM 0.670
— i.e. **before the one-moment fit the model misses the HK 0.85 literature target by −26.9pp**.

**Reading.** The single fitted moment cannot pin the cross-country shadow pattern: matching HK's 0.85
forces PH and KHM to ~88–89% take-up, for which there is **no verified literature target** (Cambodia:
Bray 2016 blocked, no verified rate; Philippines: no verified tutoring target). The model therefore
"matches" the one literature number only by making the shadow sector near-universal everywhere. Take-up
by country is **not identified** from the available moments.

## 5. Target misses (observed cells only; targets are not fitted)

- PH: upper-secondary completion 0.661 vs **0.750** (GMR observed 2024) → miss **−0.089**.
- HK: senior-secondary tutoring 0.865 vs **0.850** (Kwo & Bray 2011, caveat) → miss +0.015 (after fit);
  uncalibrated miss **−0.269**. Degree proxy: complex-job share 0.352 vs post-sec attainment 0.364
  (degree 0.276) — proxy only, not directly comparable.
- KHM: completion 0.378 vs **0.279** (GMR observed 2023) → **+0.099**; formal-job share 0.257 vs
  **0.117** (CLFS 2019) → **+0.140**; university-attainment proxy (complex-job share) 0.241 vs **0.066**
  → **+0.175** (the largest number in the table, proxy definition declared).

**Biggest miss against the literature shadow-education targets:** the uncalibrated HK senior-secondary
take-up is **−26.9pp** below the caveated 85% target; after the single-moment fit the HK miss becomes
+1.5pp but the model then puts PH/KHM at ~88–89% with no literature support, so the cross-country shadow
pattern is unidentified. The biggest absolute data-target miss overall is KHM's formal/university
attainment proxies (+14.0pp / +17.5pp), which points at the formal-sector parameterisation, not the
shadow sector.

## 6. Positional response test (shadow education as positional response)

Within-trajectory, take-up when college-gate competition R_C is above vs below its median: PH +0.032,
HK −0.003, KHM −0.004; 2sd of take-up 0.257 / 0.212 / 0.242 → **not distinguishable in any country**.
In this specification tutoring demand responds to returns, prices and capacity, **not detectably to gate
competition**. The positional channel is not identified here; treat the paper's positional claim as
untested by this module, not confirmed.
