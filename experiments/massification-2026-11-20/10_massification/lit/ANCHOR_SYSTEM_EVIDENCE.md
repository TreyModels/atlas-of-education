# ANCHOR_SYSTEM_EVIDENCE — how one system massified, mechanism by mechanism

**Anchor = PHILIPPINES.** Stated as an assumption to be confirmed, with reasons: (i) it is the worked case in both previous manuscripts; (ii) it has a **dated policy event** (RA 10533, 2013) whose predecessor is a 64-year non-event; (iii) it is the only one of the six with a **full completion series** (primary/lower/upper secondary, 1999–2024) in data-hunter's batch; (iv) it has an informal-formal margin and a migration channel, i.e. the two mechanisms the before-phase established. **Portability tests: CHN (full series, different state capacity) and HKG (no completion series; enrolment only).** KOR/JPN are treated as near-ceiling benchmarks, not anchors.

## 0. The system record the model must match (OBSERVED, data-hunter `UNIVERSALIZATION_SERIES.csv`, UIS provenance)

| Indicator | 1999 | 2013 | 2024 | Crossing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary completion | 83.2 | — | **98.3** | ≥90 in **2017** (91.9); ≥95 in **2021** (95.4) |
| Lower-secondary completion | **58.9** | — | **92.4** | ≥90 only in **2024** (92.4) |
| Upper-secondary completion | **59.7** | — | **75.0** | **never ≥90**; still 75.0 in 2024 |
| Tertiary GER | 16.3 (1971) | — | 53.0 (2026) | ≥50 in **2025** (51.1); ≥15 already in 1971 (16.3) |

**This is the single most important fact for the anchor design: the Philippines universalized *primary* (2017-21) and *lower-secondary* (2024) but has NOT universalized *upper-secondary* (75% and rising slowly).** A model that reproduces "universalization" as one curve cannot get this pattern — the levels cross decades apart and the top level has not crossed. **The anchor target is therefore a *level-ordering with gaps*, not a single crossing year.**

## 1. State push: law, places, fees, teachers

| Date | Event | What changed | Citation / access |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1940** | Education Act eliminates a grade (the ten-year cycle's origin, as a temporary cost measure) | cycle shortened to 10 years | STANDARD-CITATION (from the October manuscript; SEPO PB 2011-02 not read) |
| **1949 onward** | UNESCO and domestic commissions recommend restoration | none funded | STANDARD-CITATION |
| **1953** | RA 896 authorises restoration | **unfunded** — no places, no teachers | STANDARD-CITATION |
| **2013** | **RA 10533** — K-12: 10→12 years, mandatory kindergarten + Senior High School | adds 2 years to the *flow*: new cohort stage, new teacher demand, new school places | STANDARD-CITATION (statute not read; dates from October table) |
| **2018** | RA 10931 — free tertiary tuition | removes price at the tertiary margin | STANDARD-CITATION |
| **1999-2024** | places/teachers: primary completion +15.1pp, lower-sec +33.5pp, upper-sec +15.3pp over the window | expansion is real but *unequal by level* | OBSERVED (completion series) |

**What the state-push account predicts and what happened.** The 2013 reform did **not** produce a jump in upper-secondary completion: 75.0 in 2024, still 15pp short of universal. The state push raised the *floor* and the *lower levels*; it did not universalize the top. That is a direct challenge to a purely legal/state account of massification.

## 2. Demand pull: returns, jobs, migration

- **Returns**: global private return to a year of schooling **8.8%/yr**, EAP **8.7%** (Psacharopoulos & Patrinos 2018, WB PRWP 8402, VERIFIED). A stable return is consistent with persistent pull but does not date the expansion.
- **Firm-side skill constraint**: only **10.1%** of Philippine firms cite inadequate workforce skills as a major obstacle (7.8% in 2009; World Bank Enterprise Surveys 2009/2015, Table 2.1 of WB 2017 PHL skills report, VERIFIED). **Weak demand-pull from skills shortage.**
- **Migration channel**: wage ratio for observably identical workers PHL→US = **3.99** (range 3.71–4.36; Clemens-Montenegro-Pritchett 2008, WPS4671, VERIFIED), and a foreign-demand shock produced **9 licensed nurses per additional migrant** with +126% licensed nurses per 100k (Abarcar & Theoharides 2024, VERIFIED). **This is the strongest pull evidence in the system — and it operates on a narrow professional minority, not the mass.**
- **Employment structure (2024, OBSERVED, data-hunter JOBS_AND_MARKETS_PANEL)**: wage-and-salaried share **63.5%**, vulnerable employment **33.9%**, agriculture **21.2%**. A third of employment is vulnerable: the formal queue the credential gates is a minority of the labour market.

## 3. Supply push: educated cohorts

- Upper-secondary completion rose 59.7→75.0 (1999-2024) while the firm skill constraint stayed at ~10% and the vulnerable-employment share stayed high: **the supply of completers grew faster than employer demand for skills.** That is the supply-push signature.
- GXL 2017 (VERIFIED model) shows how supply can outrun demand endogenously via provider reinforcement; the shadow-phase models produced a graduate-supply shock with diploma-holder income falling — but those are **model outputs, not observations of the Philippines.**

## 4. Portability tests (what must transfer)

| Test | CHN | HKG |
|---|---|---|
| Completion series available | **YES** (primary 99.0 in 2000; lower-sec crossed ≥90 in **2016** (90.6), 95.7 in 2020; upper-sec 44.0 (2010) → 54.8 (2012) → **87.5 (2020)**; tertiary GER ≥15 in **2004**, ≥50 in **2017**) | **NO completion series** (UIS gap); enrolment only |
| Dated policy event | **1986 Compulsory Education Law** (nine-year); 2006 revision; 2021 Double Reduction | **1971** free primary; **1978** nine-year compulsory; **2008/09** 3-3-4; **2012** first HKDSE |
| Structural difference | state capacity high, single-party, hukou segmentation | city-economy, migration-driven, no agriculture |
| Prediction that must hold if the mechanism is portable | lower-sec ≥90 should follow the law with a lag (~2 decades for CHN: 1986→2016) | primary universalization should follow the 1971/1978 reforms within ~5 years (observed: NER ≥90 in 1974) |

**Portability verdict in advance:** the *timing* mechanism that looks portable is **law → places → completion with a two-decade lag** (CHN 1986→2016; PHL 2013→ still incomplete, consistent with a longer lag at lower capacity). The mechanism that is **not** portable is migration-driven demand (HKG has no agricultural surplus to push out; PHL's pull is a professional minority).

## 5. What is missing for the anchor (post to data-hunter)
1. **PHL place expansion series** (public secondary schools, SHS places 2015-2024) and **teacher supply** (DepEd hiring) — the mechanical channel between law and completion.
2. **PHL wage/returns by education** (PSA FIES/LFS) — blocked (PSA 403); the single biggest gap.
3. **PHL informality series** — blocked (ILOSTAT 403); currently only KHM 2019 CLFS observed.
4. **Historical completion pre-1999** — the series starts 1999, so the earlier expansion (1960s-90s) is unobserved.
