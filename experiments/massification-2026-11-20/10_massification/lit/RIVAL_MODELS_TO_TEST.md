# RIVAL_MODELS_TO_TEST — six accounts, one system record

Each account below states: mechanism, the **timing trace** it predicts, the **observable signature** in the PHL record, the **discriminating test**, and the **falsifier**. Only falsifiers that the record can actually decide are listed as decisive; the rest are marked "needs data".

## The one decisive test the PHL record can run now
**The 2013 K-12 reform is a natural experiment on the state-push channel.** It raised the required years and created a new terminal stage. State-push accounts predict a step change in the *stage structure* and a rise in completion; observed: **upper-secondary completion 75.0 in 2024, still not universal**, and lower-secondary crossing 90 only in 2024. So the state push moved the *floor and the lower levels* but did not universalize the top. **Models 1-3 and 6-B survive this; a naive 6-A (demand-led) and a naive 4 (returns-driven) do not explain why the top level stalled.**

## What the runner must predeclare
1. Which measure defines a crossing (completion vs GER vs NER) — they differ by up to a decade.
2. The fit/holdout split: **fit 1999-2013, holdout 2014-2024** (the reform is the break; using post-2013 data to fit makes the policy test circular).
3. Which rival is scored on *ordering and gaps* vs which on *levels* — only ordering/gaps discriminate.
4. The tolerance: Council A4 has adopted **±5 years** for crossing-year claims.
