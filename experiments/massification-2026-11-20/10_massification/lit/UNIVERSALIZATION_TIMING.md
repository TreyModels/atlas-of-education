# UNIVERSALIZATION_TIMING — when Japan, Korea and Hong Kong actually universalized

> **Companion (canonical evidence side):** `UNIVERSALIZATION_TIMELINES_JPN_KOR_HKG.md` supplies the dated crossing tables with measure definitions and access status (requested by repo-auditor under amendment A4). This file keeps the **rival-theory timing predictions** (§4) and the mechanism discussion; the two are cross-linked and must not drift.

**Why this file exists.** For JPN/KOR/HKG universalization is *already achieved*; the research question is **when each level crossed**, because the crossing year is the dependent variable that discriminates the rival theories. Trow's phases and MMI are ratio-driven and say little about calendar time; world society, human capital, push-pull and state-building make *different* timing predictions. This file supplies the crossings and then states what each theory predicts about them.

## 0. Method and sourcing

Two independent layers, deliberately kept apart:
- **Layer 1 — DERIVED crossings.** I computed first-crossing years directly from `04_data/data/gmr_observed_6countries_1970_2024.csv` (data-hunter's UIS/GMR panel). This gives verifiable, reproducible crossing years **but only for 1970 onward**. Every derived number states the series and the threshold.
- **Layer 2 — historical anchors.** Pre-1970 dates and the institutional causes come from the standard literature and, where I could read it, from primary/institutional pages. These are labelled STANDARD-CITATION and must be checked against MEXT/KEDI/EDB originals before publication.

**Three measurement warnings that change the answers:**
1. **GER vs NER vs ANER.** Gross ratios exceed 100% (over-age, repeaters, foreign students); net ratios understate participation where over-age enrolment is common. HKG upper-secondary GER reaches 106.1 (2019) while NER is far lower. **A crossing year is only meaningful with the measure named.**
2. **Series disagreement.** `04_data/data/gmr_source_disagreements.csv` exists because GMR and UIS series disagree. The HKG lower-secondary anomaly below (ANER crossing 90% only in 2014, despite 9-year compulsory schooling from 1978) is almost certainly a measure/denominator artefact, not a real 36-year delay. **Do not read the derived HKG lower-secondary crossing as history.**
3. **Overage and grade repetition** inflate ANER in systems with late entry; this is why ANER and NER cross 90% decades apart for the same country.

**Mechanisms the literature credits for Japan's timing.**
- **State-building first:** Meiji 1872/1900/1907 sequence is the canonical state-formation case (Gellner/Tilly logic): schooling as conscript/administrative infrastructure, imposed before industrial demand for skills.
- **Industrialization demand second:** postwar upper-secondary and tertiary expansion tracks the high-growth era and the exam-competition system.
- **Exam competition:** the entrance-examination complex is credited with sustaining demand *after* universalization (the shadow-education literature's Japan case).
- **Fertility:** cohort decline from the 1970s is credited with raising per-child investment and sustaining tertiary expansion after 1990.

**Mechanisms credited.**
- **State-building after war:** compulsory primary follows the Korean War and the 1949 Education Law; the 1954 plan is a reconstruction/legibility project.
- **Industrialization demand:** export-led industrialisation (1960s-70s) pulls secondary expansion; the 1969 middle-school equalisation reform is both a demand response and a political equalisation move.
- **Exam competition:** the CSAT and tiered university system sustain demand after universalization (Kang 2026 estimates 77%/89% of tutoring spend is competition-driven — the *post*-universalization regime).
- **Fertility:** the world's sharpest cohort decline (TFR ~0.7) is the strongest case for the cohort-size mechanism; the literature asserts, but I did not verify, that per-child investment rose.

**Mechanisms credited.**
- **State-building/order, not demand:** the 1966-67 disturbances are the standard trigger story for the 1971 primary and 1978 compulsory-education reforms — this is the Paglayan (post-conflict order) mechanism, and HKG is the cleanest instance in the set because there was no agricultural/industrial transition to blame.
- **Industrialisation/structural shift:** the 1970s-80s manufacturing-to-services transition pulls secondary expansion.
- **Exam competition:** HKDSE/JUPAS sustains the shadow sector after universalization (secondary in this phase; see the closed shadow close-out).
- **Cohort decline + emigration:** post-2010s demographic contraction and emigration are the live drivers of the current phase.

## 4. What each rival theory predicts about the **crossing year**

The point is not the level — all versions of every theory can accommodate "it ended up universal". The point is **which observable should move the crossing year earlier or later**, and what would falsify that.

| Theory | Timing prediction (which driver moves the crossing year) | Discriminating observable | Falsifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trow (phases)** | Crossing years are not a prediction at all — the ratio thresholds (15%/50%) are definitional. Trow predicts **behaviour changes at the ratio**, whenever it is reached. | Discontinuity in selection/curriculum/attrition *at the ratio*, independent of calendar year and country | Smooth behaviour through 15%/50% with no regime change |
| **World society (Schofer & Meyer)** | Crossings **synchronise across countries by date**, not by income; early for states deeply embedded in world-society ties; expansion continues where returns are weak | Similar crossing **dates** at very different GDP p.c.; crossings clustering around international declarations | Crossings tracking income or returns with little cross-national synchrony |
| **MMI (Raftery & Hout)** | Crossing year of level *k* is set by **saturation of level k−1**; the advantaged cross first, so "the crossing year" differs by class — a national series hides the class-specific dates | Class-specific crossing years ordered advantaged-first; inequality falling only after saturation | Inequality falling during expansion before saturation; classes crossing simultaneously |
| **Human capital (Becker/Mincer)** | Crossing moves **earlier when returns rise and later when costs rise**; crossing should lag the return by the payoff-perception lag | Crossing year correlates with the return with a lag; earlier where the wage premium is larger | Expansion while returns fall (the programme's anomaly) |
| **Push-pull: demand-led** | Crossing **follows** job/sector growth — e.g. Japan's high-growth era, Korea's export industrialisation, HK's manufacturing boom | Job growth leads enrolment (Granger), with a lag | Enrolment leading job growth |
| **Push-pull: supply-led** | Crossing **precedes or is independent of** job growth; expansion is institutional/state-led | Enrolment leading; crossings during low demand | Crossings tightly following demand with no independent push |
| **State-building (Gellner/Tilly/Paglayan)** | Crossing follows **state capacity or internal conflict**: Meiji after 1868, Korea after 1950-53, HK after 1966-67 | Crossings clustered after wars/disturbances; earlier where state capacity is higher | Crossings uncorrelated with capacity/conflict once income controlled |
| **Fertility/cohort** | Cohort decline **raises per-child investment** and accelerates per-child (not per-capita) crossing; may accelerate *later* levels | Cohort size predicts per-child investment and the *per-child* crossing year | No cohort-size effect on per-child investment |

**The key discriminating insight.** The three cases accidentally provide a **natural experiment on the trigger**: Japan crossed primary under state-building *before* industrial demand; Korea crossed after war and *with* export industrialisation; Hong Kong crossed after civil disturbance with *no* agrarian transition. If crossings were demand-led, HKG (no industrial take-off at the 1971/1978 reforms) should not have universalised when it did. If crossings were state-led, Japan's 1872-1907 sequence is the canonical case and Korea's 1954 plan fits. **The pattern in the literature favours state-building/order shocks as the trigger, with demand shaping the *upper* levels.** That is a testable claim, not an established one — the derived crossings above plus a conflict/capacity event file would test it.
