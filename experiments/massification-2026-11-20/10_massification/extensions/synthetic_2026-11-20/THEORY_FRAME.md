# THEORY FRAME — the programme's combined theory (candidate frame; THEORY ONLY, never evidence)

**Status: DECLARED-THEORY. Not evidence, not a model layer, no parameters.** This file collects the theory frame the
programme now carries and the **explicit honesty bounds** on it. It combines: the two PI working papers (hysteresis —
`08_council/brief_019/`; developmental assembly — `08_council/brief_020/THEORY_FRAME.md`), Council rulings D98–D105,
and two user formulations recorded 2026-11-20 (D124 bimodal system; D125 one-way ratchet). Every claim below is
**theory-only** unless marked otherwise. No causal or direction claim is licensed; no rejection of either paper is
implied.

---

## 1. The macro-principle: the ONE-WAY RATCHET ("there is no reverse button")

**The user's frame (recorded, D125).** On the development/industrialisation path, **nobody ever says "go back."** Huge
shocks (budget crises, school closures) produce **setbacks, not reversal to the prior configuration**. The options
themselves are **path-created**: the live choice is "build wafer fabs" vs "keep exporting labour", not "return to the
prior state". A Polanyi-like one-way transformation — **but stronger**: the double movement happens *inside* the
installed state, not back to the old one. **The illustrative examples (Manila → hill tribe) are thought experiments and
must NOT be modelled literally.**

**Status: DECLARED THEORY CLAIM (theory-only, never evidence).** Three declared corollaries:
1. **Transition rules are irreversible by declaration** — the model carries **no reverse/reversal term**.
2. **Shocks may set back levels but do not reverse the path** — shocks enter only as **declared inputs**.
3. **The option set is path-created** — what is available now depends on what was installed before.

**Relation to the papers.** This is **stronger** than the hysteresis paper's exit-cost asymmetry: exit cost is *why*
the ratchet holds, not the ratchet itself. It is consistent with both papers — scaffold adoption → no exit; schooling
installs competences → the option set changes.

## 2. The structural point: BIMODAL-GATE (D124; Amendment A5)

**The user's correction (recorded):** the Philippine pattern is **bimodal within the same system** — low-cost, if not
free, seats go **untaken** by families choosing out of education at upper-secondary (starting at lower-secondary), **at
the same time as extreme competition at the gates**. That coexistence is the object. **"Demand falsified" is banned
language**: demand is revealed and strong (shadow education, queues for good schools, closure reactions, credential
chasing). The A3-D result is **"the unrationed single-gate model cannot represent a bimodal system"** — a
**model-specification failure**.

**Two tiers (v1.5):** (Tier O) open-access low-cost/low-quality seats that can go unfilled — take-up is an equilibrium
between quality, opportunity cost and the option value of work; (Tier H) rationed high-quality gates — good SHS tracks,
university slots, **credentialed jobs (the model's rung-4 absorption ≈ 0.151 for the PHL-like type, not the unrationed
school gate ≈ 0.999)** — with queues, rank, exams and shadow competition. Explicit outside option: the v1.2 childworld
four-state block (school / both / work / neither; out-of-school defaults to work). Families sort by income / ability /
taste. Returns differ by tier.

## 3. Tier-specific exit cost (a sharpening, for the Council)

Exiting the **low-quality open seat is cheap — and people do it** (seats go untaken). Exiting the **credential gate is
expensive — and nobody does** (the PHL ceiling never crosses 90). That is **more testable** than "exit cost" as a single
quantity, and it is a **WT-adjacent** question (Council to consider; **not** a re-opening of D101 — no exit-cost layer
until WT1 ∧ WT3).

## 4. What the data actually show — and what they do not

**Observed setbacks without reversal (bounded, descriptive):**
- PHL upper-secondary completion 2017 → 2022: **−8.16 pp**, then recovery to **75.03** by 2024 — still **never ≥90**.
- WT4 (D108): **zero sustained exit episodes** — the ceiling does not revert toward its predecessor level through the
  K-12 / free-tuition / cohort shocks. **Descriptive and fragile** (2023 clears the 5 pp trigger by 0.496 pp; 2024
  clears the recovery band by 0.592 pp).
- **This is an observable implication, NOT a test of the strong claim.** "Observed setbacks ≠ reversal" is what the
  data support. **The strong claim — "nobody ever goes back" — is untestable on our data** (no withdrawal episodes, no
  pre-reform configuration panel), and no reversal diagnostic may be proposed as if it were reachable. Any future
  reversal test must be predeclared and labelled **descriptive**.

**What the theory frame is NOT:** it is not evidence; it is not validated by WT/DT (both conditional gates closed null:
exit-cost layer PARKED, induction grounding PARKED); it does not license a model layer; and no synthetic PASS can be
read as confirmation of it.
