# JPN - Japan (country card)

**Headline for the model: the participation *level* is well measured (~42% of sixth graders) but the widely circulated time-series is disputed - handle with care.**

## 1. Shadow education

| Measure | Value | Year | Source (access) | Denominator |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Juku attendance, Grade 6, mean across prefectures** | **41.8%** | c.2019 (national achievement test + juku data) | Rappleye & Komatsu, *Is Shadow Education the Driver of East Asia's High Performance on Comparative Learning Assessments?*, EPAA 28(67), 2020, Table 2 (FULL-TEXT, `raw/rappleye_komatsu2020_epaa_japan.pdf`) | sixth graders, all prefectures |
| Juku attendance range across prefectures | **22.1% (Akita) - 57.9% (Tokyo)** | same | Rappleye & Komatsu 2020, Table 2 (FULL-TEXT) | sixth graders by prefecture |
| Grades 1-4 taking **math** lessons | **15.0%** | same | Rappleye & Komatsu 2020 (FULL-TEXT) | grades 1-4 |
| Grades 1-4 taking **science** lessons | **0.9%** | same | Rappleye & Komatsu 2020 (FULL-TEXT) | grades 1-4 |
| Juku participation, primary (1985 -> 2007) | **16% -> 26%** | 1985-2007 | **OECD 2012, *Lessons from PISA for Japan*, p. 72, Fig. 2.2.4** - quoted *and disputed* by Rappleye & Komatsu 2020 | primary students |
| Juku participation, lower secondary (1985 -> 2007) | **44% -> 53%** | 1985-2007 | same OECD 2012 figure, quoted *and disputed* | lower-secondary students |

**Why the disputed series matters.** Rappleye & Komatsu quote the OECD sentence "At the upper secondary level, participation in private tutoring is even greater" and then state it is "factually inaccurate, even blatantly so", showing in their Fig. 1 that upper-secondary private tutoring is *lower*, not higher. **So: do not use "upper-secondary juku > lower-secondary" as a target.** If a time-series target is needed, use the OECD 1985-2007 primary/lower-secondary pair *with a "contested" flag*, or use the point-in-time 41.8% grade-6 rate (cleaner).

## 2. Gates
- **High-school entrance examinations** (prefectural, competitive in urban areas) - this is the gate that juku demand is normally attached to.
- **University entrance**: National Center for University Entrance Examinations test -> replaced by the **Common Test for University Admissions from 2021** (policy date widely documented; **primary document not retrieved this session**).
- Pass rates, applicant ratios by prefecture: **NOT FOUND** this session.

## 3. Cohort flows
- Upper-secondary and tertiary transition rates: **NOT FOUND** this session.
- Verified contextual fact used by Rappleye & Komatsu: the "exam hell" pressure narrative is itself contested - they argue learning time and exam stress have declined in Japan. This is a **literature disagreement that directly bears on the model's demand specification**.

## 6. Disagreements / unverifiable
- **The OECD versus Rappleye-Komatsu dispute is the key disagreement in this card.** The manuscripts should not cite the OECD juku series as settled fact.
- Prefecture-level juku data are not a national participation rate; 41.8% is an unweighted mean across prefectures unless the source says otherwise - check weighting before calibration.
- No income-quintile juku gradient was found. Japan's is the weakest card for equity data in this set.
