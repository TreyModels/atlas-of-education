# KHM - Cambodia (country card)

**Headline for the model: the shadow sector here is *teacher-sold*, and it is the highest participation share in the set at the Grade-9 gate.**

**NOT FOUND:** any nationally representative tutoring take-up rate for Cambodia; any Ministry of Education monitoring of the *rien kuo* market; any post-2015 replication.
**Useful negative result:** the **SEA-PLM 2024 Cambodia National Report** (104 pp., `raw/seaplm2024_cambodia_national_report.pdf`) contains **no shadow-education module** - a full-text search for "tutor / extra class / private class / additional lesson" returns nothing. So SEA-PLM cannot supply a Cambodia target; do not invent one from it.

**Provider composition.** Dominant provider is the **own teacher** selling the regular curriculum back to the same students (*rien kuo*), not a commercial chain. This is the empirically strongest case for Jayachandran's deliberate-withholding branch (`MODEL_CARDS.md` §4) and the weakest case for "commercial tutoring industry".

## 2. Gates
- **Grade 9 transition examination** - the gate the June/October manuscripts call decisive for formal-labor-market entry; the Brehm-Silova sample is built around exactly this cohort.
- **Grade 12 (upper secondary) examination** - terminal school credential.
- Pass rates by year: **NOT FOUND** this session.
- Public examination content/marking: monthly teacher-set subject tests (unstandardised, unmonitored) determine class ranking; Khmer and mathematics carry 100 points vs 50 for other subjects - this *is* the gate architecture that channels tutoring demand (Brehm & Silova WP39, §4.1).

## 3. Cohort flows
- Upper-secondary dropout: **~21%** of enrolled students (UNESCO 2010, quoted in Brehm & Silova WP39, SECOND-HAND).
- Education spending: **2.3% of GDP** (Brehm & Silova WP39, SECOND-HAND from UNESCO/EFA).
- Completion, transition and repetition rates by stage: **NOT FOUND** this session.

## 4. Economy context
- Teacher salary ~US$44/month at the time of the Benveniste fieldwork, "difficult (if not impossible)" to cover necessities - the fiscal origin of teacher-sold tutoring (Brehm & Silova WP39 p. ~5, SECOND-HAND).
- Formal/informal employment shares, wage premia, migration channels: **NOT VERIFIED** this session (see LITERATURE_LEDGER §C; the CLFS/CSES sources were on the data plan and are another agent's task).

## 5. Policy timeline (verified items only)
- Fee-free basic education policy coexists with a large paid tutoring sector - the "fee-free script on a household-funded system" pattern both manuscripts describe.
- Dates for the Grade 9/12 exam reforms, the 2014 teacher-pay reform, and any tutoring regulation: **NOT FOUND** this session.

## 6. Disagreements / unverifiable
- **Sample generalisability is the central limitation**: 282 Grade-9 students in two schools. Brehm & Silova are explicit that this is not national. The 68.4% figure is a *case-study* rate and must be labelled as such in the model, not as a national participation rate.
- Whether teacher-sold tutoring is coercion or service provision is genuinely disputed; the paper itself distinguishes the two and does **not** claim all teachers withhold.
- The Bray et al. (2016) Cambodia article and the Brehm & Silova *published* version (JERO 6(1)) were **not retrievable** (403 / not located); all Cambodia numbers above come from the working-paper version, which is close but not guaranteed identical.
