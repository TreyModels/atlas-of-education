# Atlas: eight survey experiments, version 1

This is a **starter analysis engine**, not an automatic DHS importer or a confirmatory study. A fleet member must build and document a country/wave-specific harmonization adapter and independently verify every field and denominator before running. Scripts have not been checked against real DHS microdata. Preserve raw files locally under their permissions; only disclose permitted, non-disclosive summaries.

Run `Rscript experiments.R normalized.csv output_dir` with R and the `survey` package. Use a separate output directory for each run. Record script and input hashes, codebook, country, wave, release, approved data access, recode, weights, strata, PSU, missing codes, sample restrictions and harmonization decisions. Use the appropriate household/person or individual-woman sample weight for each row, not a single weight across both. Survey PSU and stratum must be the real design variables; ID values must be unique within strata. Sampling design is handled separately per country/wave/record type. The code fails on singleton PSUs: investigate actual design and document an appropriate treatment rather than quietly changing options.

Input CSV columns: country, wave, weight, stratum, psu, record_type (`person` or `woman`), age, sex, urban (1 urban/0 rural), wealth (1–5, survey-specific relative index), attendance (1/0), school_years, children_ever_born, age_first_birth, currently_in_school (1/0), age_first_union, household_size. Include columns even where missing. Encode age_first_birth/union as 99 only for known never; NA for unknown. Never treat an unknown as no birth or no union. Household sizes refer to current household, not kin network. Validate distributions and recodings against published survey reports. No auto-combination of repeated cross-sections, no assumed person links.

E1 attendance by wealth; E2 by residence; E3 by sex (ages 14–17); E4 children ever born by years of schooling (women 40–49); E5 birth before age 20 by schooling (women 25–34); E6 current school participation by residence (women 20–24); E7 union before age 20 by schooling (women 25–34); E8 current household size by schooling (women 40–49). All are within-wave descriptive domain means with design-based SEs, not causal estimates. Age and schooling precede survey but measured schooling may occur after early birth/union: these comparisons do not establish temporal or causal direction. E8 is not a measure of kin support. Completed-cohort fertility is subject to selection/survival and survey eligibility. Wealth quintiles are not income, and pooled population group shares need not equal 40% within an age domain. Small cells, missingness, comparability, multiple testing and multiple countries require clear reporting; suppress unreliable/disclosive cells, compare with official benchmarks, and obtain independent replication. Normal CIs are approximate, not an inferential claim about a mechanism.

Missing areas: agricultural prices, actual migration histories, education/tutoring expenditure, employment wages, and schooling completion require verified separate data sources or variables; none is silently inferred here. The models' exits, attendance and completion are distinct observables. Use only compatible definitions, inspect the prior audit on model identifiability, and retain genuine out-of-sample observations. Future designs for migration/agriculture and the credential-return puzzle should be authored after inventory of sources, geography, timing and feasible counterfactuals. Do not claim a fertility effect of schooling or adjudicate Domus based on these correlations.
