## What this establishes

1. **Selected on training fit alone** (new-method 2024 MAE, ties broken by old-method fit): **spread8**. Test MAE 0.99 against naive 0.89: the training-fit winner does not beat the naive benchmark on the test months. It is 0.10 pts worse.
2. **Which variants beat naive.** On test MAE (naive 0.89), 2 of 5 variants are below naive: spread11, duration+spread11 (0.71-0.77). Not below naive: base, duration, spread8 (0.99-1.71). Of the variants with spread-out entry (spread8, spread11, duration+spread11), spread11, duration+spread11 beat naive and spread8 does not. Variants without it: base (1.71), duration (1.19). Every spread variant has lower test MAE than the base model, which is consistent with, but does not establish, the reading that a large share of each graduating class enters the measured labour market over the following academic year rather than in July (for example through postgraduate and civil-service exam calendars). That reading is a hypothesis; the test months cannot tell it apart from other ways of flattening the seasonal shape.
3. **Strength of the evidence.** 19 test months; against naive the selected variant (spread8) has a mean paired difference of +0.10 pts (negative favours the model), wins 7/19 months, paired t +0.66. This is not a statistically distinguishable advantage over naive. Its July error is +1.4 pts. Each new NBS release extends the test, and the claim should be rescored then, without refitting.
4. **This is not a blind test.** The 5 variants were fixed before these fits ran, and the winner is chosen by training fit. But the candidate mechanisms came from forecast_2024.md, which had already shown the test-period seasonal errors next to the fit-window ones. The same pattern is visible within the 2024 fit window alone, so the diagnosis did not need the test months, but they had been seen. The first blind check is the next NBS releases (Oct 2026 onwards), scored with the parameters frozen in seasonal_2024.json.
