## What this establishes

1. **A demand story is needed, but it is graduate-specific.** Matching 2023-26 needs graduate-job demand about 6% below the 2022 rule in 2023, falling to 15% below by 2025-26. The aggregate version needs economy-wide demand 4-8% lower, which through the model's own adult free-entry condition implies adult unemployment rising towards 6%. The observed overall urban rate, which bounds the adult rate from above, stayed at 5.1-5.2%. The aggregate story is contradicted.
2. **The vacancy failure is explained, not fixed.** In this model vacancies are posted in proportion to searchers (free entry), so more unemployed graduates mean more postings. A graduate-demand path that matches unemployment gives postings +4% in H1 2025, against -22% observed. One demand series cannot fit both, so either job-board postings are not the model's vacancies (coverage, platform shifts), or matching efficiency fell, or more graduates stopped searching (a NEET margin). Each is testable.
3. **Even with demand free, the model loses to naive** (MAE about 2, against 1.61), because of seasonality, not level. Under the new NBS definition the model overshoots the July graduation jump by about 4 pts and undershoots spring by 2-3 pts. Graduation-season entry (`july_g`, student search `zeta`) was fitted to old-method data, where searching students were counted. The new-method seasonal shape is a separate calibration problem from the demand question.
4. **Wages:** the graduate path leaves held-out 2022-23 starting wages about 10 log points too high. Lower demand should cut graduate pay, but the model's wages respond too little.
