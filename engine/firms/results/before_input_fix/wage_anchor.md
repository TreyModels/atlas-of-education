## What this establishes

1. Starting wages cannot pin lam_g in this model: with g_H fixed from 2010-18, no wage speed reproduces the steady erosion of the graduate premium, because the model has none before 2021.
2. Freeing the graduate demand drift lets the model match 2019-21 wages, but only with demand for graduates shrinking by about 7% a year (the shift is at its bound), and it then overshoots the held-out 2022-23 wages.
3. With wages matched, the forecast is as bad as before (5.6 vs naive 1.7). In this model falling wages clear the graduate queue. In China, graduate wages fell AND youth unemployment stayed high. The two-type model cannot produce that combination; a state that keeps graduates out of the job queue while wages fall (waiting for exams or secure posts: the Tilly waiting pool) is the next candidate.
