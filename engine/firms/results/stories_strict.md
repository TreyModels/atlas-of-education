## What this establishes

1. **Out of sample the tournament winner does not beat the like-for-like naive benchmark:** 1.09 against 0.89 pts (t +0.89 over 19 months).
2. **Against the simpler spread-out-entry model** (0.77): the extra mechanisms do not improve the forecast.
3. **The postings fall is still not reproduced:** -89% against -22%, close to the in-sample tournament figure (-10%), so the strict protocol did not change this failure.
3b. **Why it fails:** the fit is good in sample (2024 new-method MAE 0.59) and then the youth rate runs away (test bias -0.41). The ablation shows no single culprit: switching off any one mechanism moves the bias by 2-9 pts, upward for credential inflation and downward for automation, the consumption loop and the 2021 step. The mechanisms are individually large and offsetting. The 2018-24 path pins down their sum, not each of them, and the sum stops working once demand is held at its last-12-month mean and the trending terms (automation, credential inflation) keep compounding.
4. **Caveat on the optimum:** 6 starts, 2 of 16 parameters at bounds. Offsetting mechanisms mean the fit surface has long flat valleys; a different point in the valley could forecast differently. The stored result is one honest strict fit, not the best the structure could do. A profile over the valley (the Monte Carlo manager's draws, scored on the test months) is the next step before calling the structure itself rejected.
5. **Not a blind test.** The mechanism set and the 11-month spread were chosen after the test months had been seen. The blind check is the Oct-Dec 2026 releases, scored with `firms.blind_forecast --score` against forecasts frozen on 5 Nov 2026.
