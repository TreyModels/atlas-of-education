## What this establishes

1. **No single story is enough.** The combined model wins decisively (BIC 236 against 278 for the best single story), even after the penalty for its extra parameters. Each single story fails on a different observable. Supply, property, credential and automation leave the graduate pay erosion unexplained (errors of about +0.2 log points). Queuing fits the exam queue and the seasonal shape but not pay. Consumption alone strains the adult labour market.
2. **Inside the combined model, a graduate-demand fall dated from August 2021 does most of the work:** +1.9 pts of 2025 youth unemployment. Then the consumption loop (+0.6) and automation (+1.0). Property loading on graduate jobs adds about +1.0; its effect runs through adult demand, already in the data. Queuing (+0.3) and credential inflation (-0.0) slightly lower the measured rate: delayed entrants are not counted, and graduates taking non-graduate jobs are employed.
3. **Read the dated step with care.** It is a fall in graduate-job value from Aug 2021. Its timing matches the tutoring ban and platform tightening, but it will also absorb any graduate-specific demand fall that began then (white-collar property and finance jobs, "jobless growth"). It identifies when and whom, not which policy. Sector-level data on graduate hiring (TODO §R) are needed to split it.
4. **Still missing:** postings fall -16% in H1 2025 in the combined model, against −22% observed. This is the second explanatory exercise to leave the postings gap open; the next mechanism to add is search and participation (NEET).
5. **This is explanation, not prediction:** every story saw 2018-26. The combined story now goes through the strict Dec 2024 protocol and the blind forecast (TODO §R, P1).
