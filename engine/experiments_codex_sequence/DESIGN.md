# MASSENG timing experiment — exploratory synthetic worlds

This design is recorded before this experiment is executed. It uses the modular `eduengine`, not the national v5 regression model. It is not fitted to a country and cannot establish how historical massification happened.

Question: within this particular model, how do timing and education-to-job feedback affect the path of a system with fiscal feedback, tuition choices, rural/urban households, employer credential queues and tutoring?

Use `worlds.formal_loop`, with FiscalState on; T=70 years, cohorts N=400, measured cohorts 35–44 after both interventions, no country enrolment anchors. In all intervention arms the education allocation of tax revenue rises from 0.18 to 0.30 and exogenous formal vacancies per entrant rise from 0.08 to 0.16. Four schedules: neither; education allocation year 15/jobs year 30; jobs year 15/education allocation year 30; both year 15. These are stipulated changes, not observed dates or plausible real-world effect sizes. Fiscal revenue is translated into school budget in the next model year, while vacancies affect that year's labour market. Employer vacancies may additionally respond to educational composition via `job_elasticity` 0 versus 1; this feedback is an assumption, not an estimate of jobs created by education. Compare quota gates at lower/upper secondary against existing absolute bar gates. Teacher withholding, tuition choices, and tutoring remain model mechanisms; any predicted tutoring spike is conditional on these rules.

Run 10 stochastic seeds per condition (160 runs); retain matched-seed contrasts with a bootstrap over seed-level differences, not individuals, as an exploratory Monte Carlo summary. Matching seeds improves comparability but is not exact common random numbers after paths diverge. Primary descriptive outputs: cohort completion by four levels, tutoring use by grade and income quintile, primary proficiency (on an assumed scale), formal GDP share, public quality, rural/urban and wealth completion parity. Track yearly formal share, education budget, quality, and tutoring so timing/lag is visible. Record and retain nonfinite or missing outputs; no causal or historical claim.

Contrasts: education-first minus jobs-first, jointly early minus no intervention, within each feedback/gate condition. Show both absolute differences and bootstrap uncertainty, without multiple-testing significance claims. Do not select parameter values or a favored contrast after observing outcomes. Model assumptions and outcome definitions are in the source, especially `eduengine/modules/state.py`, `labour.py`, and `sdg4.py`. Quota gates and job elasticities are structural scenarios, not empirically estimated effects. Realistic data fit, historical event dates, migration flows, independently observed places, tutoring costs/distribution and employer credential requirements are not implemented. Rurality here is a household attribute and extra schooling cost; migration is not simulated.
