How the model works

Country enrollment follows the change in age-band population from its observed 2025 anchor. The reference keeps the starting pupil–teacher ratio for staffing targets and applies a 0.45 elasticity to school operations. The retention case uses 2025 staffing targets and operations as minimums. Faster consolidation uses elasticities of 1.2 for staffing and 0.85 for operations. A smaller pupil–teacher ratio can make smaller classes possible. It also allows more specialist or support time. Hong Kong uses modeled classes in the city network instead. City capacity policies are defined in the network detail.

In each year, staff first leave at the selected exit rate. The model then fills openings to reach the staffing target where necessary. Incumbents above target are kept by default. The checkbox converts those excess posts into modeled involuntary separations. Each combined Hong Kong stage is staffed independently before aggregation.

Training starts enter the candidate pool after the selected lag, adjusted for qualification and application. For stages with an existing intake series, a custom intake changes new cohorts starting in 2027. Where the series is missing, the custom number also fills earlier unobserved cohorts for illustration. The costs cover annual teacher payroll in the chosen currency. The model contains no estimate of learning outcomes or building costs.

UN World Population Prospects 2024 provides country age counts and the medium projection. Age bands are proxies for school participation. The annual output uses the calendar year in which the school year begins.

Data and code are embedded. The scenario runs locally in the browser.
