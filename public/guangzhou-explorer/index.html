Can talented pupils from poorer families reach a selective university in Guangzhou?

This is a simulation study of educational opportunity. We modeled 40,000 synthetic pupils in each of 1,024 sets of assumptions about families, schools and tutoring, then compared who secured a fixed number of university proxy places.

We used Guangzhou admissions rules and published statistics on school places and boarding capacity to inform the model. For inputs we cannot yet measure well, like how much tutoring helps and how long early advantages last, we tested a range of assumptions. The charts show what the simulated pupils did under those settings. Better data on tutoring, boarding and how pupils progress over time would help narrow the ranges and test the results.

    Sources, citation & data provenance

    The principal admissions experiment spans 1,024 parameter settings with 40,000 synthetic pupils each. The revised tutoring-choice experiment spans 40 settings plus 2 legacy benchmarks across 8 paired 40,000-pupil cohorts (seeds 2201–2208). Both studies use synthetic cohorts to isolate mechanisms under stated assumptions.
