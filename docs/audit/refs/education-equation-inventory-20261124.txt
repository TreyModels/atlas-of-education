Master Equation Inventory: Economics of Education, Education and Development, and Shadow Education
This is a research-oriented inventory of economic equations, mathematical models, and econometric specifications used across education economics.

I'll organize them by where they belong in an academic paper, thesis, dissertation, or research framework, along with what each equation is designed to explain.

The inventory covers foundational human capital theory, educational investment, school production, household decisions, labor-market returns, private tutoring, inequality, economic growth, public finance, and causal identification.

There is no finite list of every possible equation because researchers develop new specifications for particular questions. The catalog below distinguishes established models from adaptable empirical specifications.

5. Economics of shadow education and private tutoring
Placement: Shadow education theoretical framework, household demand analysis, private tutoring market analysis, empirical methodology.

This is the specialized section for shadow education. The literature studies private tutoring as a household expenditure, an educational input, a competitive investment, and a potential contributor to inequality.

The foundational literature includes Dang and Rogers (2008), Bray, Tansel and Bircan, and more recent empirical studies using hurdle models.

6. Education, human capital, and economic development
Placement: Education and development theoretical framework, macroeconomic methodology, economic growth analysis.

These models examine the relationship between education, skills, economic productivity, technological adoption, and long-run growth.

The established foundations include Mankiw, Romer and Weil's augmented Solow model, Lucas's human-capital model, Nelson and Phelps's diffusion mechanism, and Hanushek and Woessmann's work on cognitive skills and growth.

Core equation sets by research question

What determines participation in shadow education?

Equations 12, 19–24, 57–70. Primary models: participation logit or probit, followed by a two-part, Tobit, or hurdle expenditure model.




Does tutoring improve learning achievement?

Equations 26–41, 71–80, 138–165. Central relationships: educational production, tutoring intensity, treatment effects, and selection.




Does shadow education increase educational inequality?

Equations 64–69, 76, 123–137, 145–158. Focus on income gradients, tutoring participation gaps, educational outcome distributions, and heterogeneous effects.




How does education contribute to economic development?

Equations 42–56, 87–105, 168–174. Use earnings equations for individual returns and human-capital growth models for macroeconomic analysis.




Should governments subsidize or regulate tutoring?

Equations 15–18, 81–86, 106–122, 141–144, 182–183. This combines policy identification, fiscal costs, welfare effects, and distributional consequences.

Important distinction for writing: Equations such as the Mincer earnings function, augmented Solow model, Gini index, and Tobit model have established mathematical forms. Many of the tutoring-specific regressions above are research specifications constructed from those foundations. They should be cited to the relevant theory and empirical design, with variable definitions and assumptions stated explicitly. A dissertation normally selects the equations needed for its hypotheses and available data rather than including the entire catalog.
