## New issue: the forecast information set

`engine/firms/model.py:demand_index` reconstructs adult unemployment from overall urban unemployment minus a fixed youth share times observed youth unemployment. Its seasonal adjustment uses the entire available period. Consequently, observed youth unemployment contributes to the demand input used to explain youth unemployment, and later observations can change earlier demand values.

Executed a perturbation audit: raised observed youth unemployment by one percentage point from 2023 onward while holding overall urban unemployment fixed. The maximum absolute change in the inferred demand index was **0.00017404378947971644 during 2018–22**, and **0.006302446841208287 from 2023 onward**. The supplied script reproduces these numbers. This establishes input dependence and backward influence; it does not quantify the effect on fitted coefficients or forecast error.

The existing early-window exercise should therefore be described as a conditional extrapolation with later observed inputs, pending a strict information-set reconstruction. Its documented failure remains informative. A clean forecast must estimate seasonal adjustment through the cutoff, use age-specific adult observations or an independent demand series, and predict or prespecify future demand inputs. Compare with a benchmark using equivalent information and compatible unemployment definitions.

# 0.9.1 — Cambodia child labour world and first Monte Carlo (4 November 2026, later)

Built on the reviewed v0.9 above. No existing model code was changed.

**Added**
- World 09, *Child labour in Cambodia*: `web/camchild.js` (engine), `web/camchild_ui.js` (view), `web/camchild_scenarios.js` (seven curated scenarios). Two linked regions, migration to Phnom Penh and Thailand, remittances and left-behind children, a ban that only inspected factory jobs feel, microfinance debt, teacher-sold extra classes, and a credential floor by region. - `tests/test_camchild.cjs` (13 tests: conservation, determinism, calibration to UIS 2024 within 2.5 points, mechanism directions), now step 9 of `run_all.py`. All ten steps pass.
- `docs/rural_economy_roadmap.md` (agricultural household, seasonal liquidity, binding constraints, financing rules, MAMS) and `docs/TODO_BY_LANE.md`.

**What the Monte Carlo changed** (median change in national child labour, last four years, share of seeds where it falls)
| Scenario | Median change | Falls in |
|---|---:|---:|
| Thai border closes | −0.13 pts | 60% |
| Garment boom | −0.10 pts | 50% |
| Factory-only ban | −0.74 pts | 100% |
| Cash for the poorest | −2.07 pts | 100% |
| The floor binds | −0.94 pts | 100% |
| Schools as protection | −6.70 pts | 100% |

A single-seed run had reported −0.8 pts for the Thai border closure. Across seeds it is indistinguishable from chance. The garment boom's net effect is also noise: it raises Phnom Penh child labour while weakening rural migration. The factory-only ban lowers rural child labour too, through a weaker pull of city factories on rural teenagers; the Bharadwaj–Lakdawala–Li displacement is not reproduced at these settings.

**Verified from the review above:** `analysis/audit_demand_information.py` reproduces its numbers (2018–22: 1.74e-4; 2023 onward: 6.30e-3). The backward effect is small; the circularity (youth unemployment feeds the demand input used to explain youth unemployment) is the substantive problem.
