# Atlas update, 5 November 2026

Picked up TODO §5 P1 (out-of-sample test against the naive benchmark) and built the strict information-set forecast that the 4 November update asked for.

- `engine/firms/forecast_strict.py`: `Vintage` data object frozen at Dec 2022. The demand index and its seasonal adjustment use pre-cutoff data only. Later inputs come from rules written down before scoring. It reports three information sets and two naive benchmarks.
- `engine/firms/test_firms.py`: two new tier-1 tests. The strict demand index does not move when post-cutoff data move, and the vintage hides the future.
- `run_all.py`: `--full` runs the strict forecast; the run report quotes it when results exist.

**Finding.** Strict forecast error 5.55 pts (new method), against 1.71 for naive. The leak identified on 4 Nov is real but immaterial (leaky 5.50). The model fails because it absorbs the 2022 graduate surge through graduate wage adjustment within about a year. The fitting window cannot identify the wage speed: holding it slower halves the error with nearly the same in-sample fit. Next step: fix it from starting-salary data, not from the test.

## Second item: graduate wage speed from starting wages

Added sourced MyCOS bachelor's starting wages (classes 2016–23) and NBS urban non-private average wages (2018–23) to `data_china.py`. `firms/wage_anchor.py` chooses lam_g from classes public by the cutoff (2018–21), then scores the strict forecast. It also runs a joint wage-and-unemployment fit with the graduate demand trend freed.

Result: the wage data reject the model's wage block rather than pin its speed. Matching wages needs graduate demand shrinking about 7% a year, and even then the forecast is 5.6 vs naive 1.7. China's combination of falling graduate wages and stubborn youth unemployment is the next target, through the categorical (Tilly) model's waiting state.

The next test asks what demand path the model would need, and whether independent data show that path.

## Fourth round: demand inversion, Python twin of the Cambodia world

**Demand inversion** (`firms/demand_inversion.py`). Parameters stay at the strict fit. I solved for the half-year demand path after 2022 that reproduces 2023–26 youth unemployment, then checked it against evidence the inversion did not use.

- The demand fall has to be graduate-specific: 6% in 2023, growing to about 15% by 2025–26.
- Economy-wide demand is contradicted by the adult labour market.
- Postings: the matching path gives +4% against −22% observed. Under free entry postings follow searchers, so one demand series cannot fit both.
- Even with demand free, seasonality keeps the error near 2. The July graduation jump is too large under the new NBS definition.

The China engine's next steps are now specific: refit the graduation season to the new definition, and find an independent graduate-demand series.

**Python twin** (`engine/camchild.py`). A line-by-line port of `web/camchild.js` that reproduces it draw for draw (9 scenarios, every choice identical). The Cambodia world can now be fitted, profiled and scored with the same tools as China.

## Fifth round: Dec 2024 cutoff, Cambodia calibration ridge

**China, Dec 2024 cutoff** (`firms/forecast_2024.py`). This is the first test using the same definition throughout. The model fits through 2024, including 13 new-method months, and forecasts 2025–26. The level is right (bias −0.09), unlike every Dec 2022 test, but the model loses to naive (1.36 vs 0.89) on seasonal amplitude. The spring months fall too far and July jumps too high. Six of 11 parameters sit at bounds. Next: the seasonal-structure mechanisms in TODO §5.

**Cambodia calibration ridge** (`engine/camchild_ridge.py`, using the Python twin). I recalibrated the six school constants under five target and split assumptions and reran the policies on five seeds.
- Child labour stays at about 14% under every calibration, so the gap from the survey is on the work side.
- Cash, the ban, the floor and schools-as-protection are robust in sign.
- The Thai closure and the garment boom have no established sign.
- UIS (17.6%) and the 2019 census (about 8.4%) disagree at lower secondary, which needs settling before any retune.

## Sixth round: seasonal mechanisms, Cambodia definitions

**China seasonal mechanisms** (`firms/seasonal.py`, `firms/seasonal_2024.py`). A docked subclass adds duration dependence and spread-out graduate entry. Under the Dec 2024 protocol, entry spread over 8–11 months beats the same-definition naive benchmark in every variant: training-selected spread11 scores 0.62 against 0.89, winning 12 of 19 months (t −1.72). This is the first China specification to beat naive out of sample. It is not blind, because the test-period errors had been seen. A blind forecast for Oct–Dec 2026 is frozen in `results/blind_forecast_2026_11.md`.

**Cambodia observation equation** (`docs/cambodia_observation_equation.md`). The model measures attendance by age, so household surveys (census 2019: 8.4% at ages 12–14; CSES 2012: 11.4%) are the matching target. UIS (17.6%, admin ÷ UN population) becomes a diagnostic. Constants are ready; the switch waits for Trey's approval.
