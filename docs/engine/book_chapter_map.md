# Book chapter map: *The Atlas of Education*

**For Bjørn Nordtveit — draft for discussion, 6 November 2026.** A one-page map, one row per chapter, tying each chapter to the world that carries it, the result it rests on, the failure or open question it must carry honestly, and the part I propose Bjørn writes or owns. Numbers are quoted only from the file named in the row. Markers: **[built]** = runs in the repo now; **[specified]** = designed, not built; **[planned]** = proposal only. Bjørn's role markers: **(C)** co-author / theory, **(M)** mechanism and magnitudes, **(F)** field and case material, **(E)** editorial and comparative framing, **(?)** to agree.

The book's argument, in one sentence: comparative education becomes cumulative when each explanation is built as a runnable mechanism inside exact accounting, confronted with patterns it was not tuned to, and allowed to fail in public — and the Atlas is that method applied to the education–labour connection across China, Cambodia, the Philippines and the cross-country record.

## What is not in the book yet — and should be a choice, not an omission

1. **No single coupled model.** China, Cambodia, childworld and the Planning Lab are separate engines with separate accounting; the two-way loop between them is specified (`docs/earlier_memos/cocoon_of_math_memo.md`) but not built. A book that claims "the Atlas" should either build the ledger or say plainly that the chapters are adjacent worlds.
2. **Sex and gender.** Absent from the household engine and from the China labour market; the massification paper's argument about care and marriage also lacks it. A chapter or an explicit limitation is needed.
3. **The observation equations.** The Cambodia UIS-vs-census gap (17.6% vs ~8.4%) and the NBS old/new definition are the best teaching cases the project has; they deserve a chapter section, not just a note.
4. **The classroom.** The Angkor lab has a six-exercise notebook and a self-contained explorer; the book should decide whether it is a methods text for students as well as a research monograph.
5. **The paper trail.** The massification paper (with Bjørn), the methods paper (Boudon/Epstein/cocoon) and this book overlap. The map should say in the preface which claim is argued where, to avoid three versions of one argument.

## Questions for Bjørn

1. Which of these chapters do you want to **own** (write), which co-write, and which merely comment on? My working assumption is C for 1, 10, 13, M for 2, 3, 4, E for 0, 6, 11, 12.
2. For chapter 4, can you give me the magnitudes for the protection package — which components, what size, with what evidence class (verified, field experience, assumption)? The model treats them as placeholders and says so.
3. Does the four-state framing (school / school-and-work / work / neither) match how you would describe the child-protection cases you know? If state D is where protection fails most, the chapter should say so.
4. The Chengdu case (chapter 9) was handed to me, not to us. Is it right to put a fieldwork chapter we cannot yet reproduce inside this book, or should it be a separate paper?
5. For the conclusion: which of the six known failures would you attack first, and would you sign a "what would change our minds" section? A failure list co-signed by both authors is stronger than one that reads as self-criticism by the model-builder alone.
